Greetings Through the Day

Goal: By the end you can greet someone at any time of day, thank them, apologise, and say goodbye politely.

Grammar and usage

Polite vs casual

おはよう is fine with friends and family. Add ございます (おはよう ございます) when talking to a teacher, a boss, a shopkeeper, or anyone you don't know well. When you're unsure, choose the polite form.

The は in こんにちは and こんばんは

These greetings are written with は, but it is read “wa”, not “ha”. They began as parts of longer sentences, and the old spelling stayed.

すみません does three jobs

It can mean “excuse me” (to get someone's attention), “sorry” (for a small mistake) and even “thank you” (when someone has gone to trouble for you). It is probably the most useful word in this lesson.

ありがとう vs ありがとうございます

ありがとう is casual. ありがとう ございます is polite. To answer thanks, say どういたしまして, or just a small smile and nod.

Culture note

  • In Japan a small nod or bow often goes with a greeting. You don't need to bow deeply, but a slight nod while saying おはよう ございます is a friendly, natural habit.
