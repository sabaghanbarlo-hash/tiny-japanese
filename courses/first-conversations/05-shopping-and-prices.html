Shopping and Prices

Goal: By the end you can ask how much something is, understand a price, and say you'll take it.

Watch the lesson

This video has no sound: watch it, then say every sound out loud yourself.

Dialogue: In a shop

  • Alex
    すみません、これは いくらですか。
    Sumimasen, kore wa ikura desu ka.
    Excuse me, how much is this?
  • Clerk
    それは さんぜん ごひゃく えんです。
    Sore wa sanzen gohyaku en desu.
    That one is 3,500 yen.
  • Alex
    ちょっと たかいですね。
    Chotto takai desu ne.
    It's a little expensive, isn't it?
  • Alex
    あれは いくらですか。
    Are wa ikura desu ka.
    How much is that one over there?
  • Clerk
    あれは にせん えんです。
    Are wa nisen en desu.
    That one is 2,000 yen.
  • Mika
    やすいですね！
    Yasui desu ne!
    It's cheap!
  • Alex
    じゃあ、あれを ください。
    Jaa, are o kudasai.
    Then I'll take that one.
  • Alex
    カードは つかえますか。
    Kaado wa tsukaemasu ka.
    Can I use a card?
  • Clerk
    はい、つかえます。ありがとう ございます。
    Hai, tsukaemasu. Arigatou gozaimasu.
    Yes, you can. Thank you.

Grammar

これ / それ / あれ

These three words point at things by distance. これ is near the speaker, それ is near the listener, あれ is far from both. In a shop, you say これ for what you're holding, and the clerk answers with それ.

X は いくらですか — “How much is X?”

いくら is “how much”. Put the item first with は, then いくらですか. This is the most useful shopping sentence.

Prices in yen

Add えん after the number: せんえん (1,000 yen), にせんえん (2,000), さんぜん ごひゃく えん (3,500). Prices follow the number rules from Lesson 3, including the exceptions for 300, 600, 800, 3,000 and 8,000.

ちょっと as a softener

ちょっと literally means “a little”. Japanese speakers use it to soften an opinion so it doesn't sound harsh: ちょっと たかいですね is a polite way of saying “that's a bit pricey.”

じゃあ — “well then”

Use じゃあ when you reach a decision based on what you just heard: じゃあ、これを ください.
