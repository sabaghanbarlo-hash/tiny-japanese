Asking for Directions

Goal: By the end you can ask where something is and follow a simple answer.

Dialogue: Where is it?

  • Alex
    すみません、コンビニは どこですか。
    Sumimasen, konbini wa doko desu ka.
    Excuse me, where is the convenience store?
  • Mika
    コンビニですか。あそこです。
    Konbini desu ka. Asoko desu.
    The convenience store? It's over there.
  • Mika
    まっすぐ いって、みぎです。
    Massugu itte, migi desu.
    Go straight, and it's on the right.
  • Alex
    まっすぐ、みぎ。わかりました。
    Massugu, migi. Wakarimashita.
    Straight, then right. Got it.
  • Alex
    あの、トイレは どこですか。
    Ano, toire wa doko desu ka.
    Um, where is the restroom?
  • Mika
    トイレは えきの ちかくです。ひだりですよ。
    Toire wa eki no chikaku desu. Hidari desu yo.
    The restroom is near the station. It's on the left.
  • Alex
    ありがとう ございます！
    Arigatou gozaimasu!
    Thank you!
  • Mika
    いいえ。
    Iie.
    Not at all.

Grammar

X は どこですか — “Where is X?”

Put the place first with は and end with どこですか. The answer repeats the pattern: X は ここです (“It's here”), あそこです (“It's over there”).

ここ / そこ / あそこ

Like これ/それ/あれ, but for places. ここ is where I am, そこ is where you are, あそこ is far from both of us.

X の ちかく — “near X”

の links two nouns, as in lesson 5 of our hiragana course: えきの ちかく means “near the station”.

まっすぐ いって — “go straight”

いって is a form of “go” meaning “go and then …”. You don't need to learn the grammar yet: just remember the phrase まっすぐ いって, みぎ / ひだり です.

よ at the end

よ adds “I'm telling you” or “by the way”. ひだりですよ means “it's on the left, you know.”. Use it to share information the other person may not know.

Useful tip

  • あの is a gentle way to get someone's attention before a question: あの、すみません。 Either word on its own, or both together, makes a good opening when you ask a stranger for help.
