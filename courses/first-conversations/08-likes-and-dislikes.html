Likes and Dislikes

Goal: By the end you can say what you like, ask others, and politely say you don't like something.

Watch the lesson

This video has no sound: watch it, then say every sound out loud yourself.

Dialogue: Favourite things

  • Mika
    アレックスさんは なにが すきですか。
    Arekkusu-san wa nani ga suki desu ka.
    What do you like, Alex?
  • Alex
    わたしは ラーメンが すきです。
    Watashi wa raamen ga suki desu.
    I like ramen.
  • Mika
    ほんとうですか！わたしも だいすきです。
    Hontou desu ka! Watashi mo daisuki desu.
    Really? I love it too.
  • Mika
    アニメは すきですか。
    Anime wa suki desu ka.
    Do you like anime?
  • Alex
    はい、だいすきです！
    Hai, daisuki desu!
    Yes, I love it!
  • Mika
    さかなは？
    Sakana wa?
    What about fish?
  • Alex
    さかなは あまり すきじゃ ないです。
    Sakana wa amari suki ja nai desu.
    I don't really like fish.
  • Mika
    そうですか。じゃあ、おにくは？
    Sou desu ka. Jaa, oniku wa?
    I see. Then what about meat?
  • Alex
    おにくは だいすきです！
    Oniku wa daisuki desu!
    I love meat!

Grammar

X が すきです — “I like X”

Use が (not を) for things you like. ラーメンが すきです means “I like ramen.” To ask: ラーメンが すきですか。 Note that the liked thing takes が in Japanese.

だいすき — “love”

だいすきです is stronger than すきです. It's used for people, food and hobbies. It's warm and natural, not a big romantic statement.

Saying you don't like it

すきじゃ ないです means “I don't like it.” Add あまり (“not very”) to soften it: あまり すきじゃ ないです. A more formal version is あまり すきじゃ ありません.

Avoid きらい

きらい means “dislike”, but it sounds strong in Japanese. It's usually better to say あまり すきじゃ ないです.

そうですか and ほんとうですか

そうですか means “I see” and ほんとうですか means “really?”. They are very common reactions that keep a conversation going.
