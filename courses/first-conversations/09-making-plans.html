Making Plans Together

Goal: By the end you can invite a friend, say yes, and arrange a time and place to meet.

Watch the lesson

Turn your sound on: listen to each sound, then say it out loud yourself. Voices: Open JTalk with the Tohoku-F01 (Tohoku University, CC BY 4.0) and Mei (Nagoya Institute of Technology, CC BY 3.0) voices.

Dialogue: Let's go to a movie

  • Mika
    アレックスさん、あした ひまですか。
    Arekkusu-san, ashita hima desu ka.
    Alex, are you free tomorrow?
  • Alex
    はい、ひまです。
    Hai, hima desu.
    Yes, I'm free.
  • Mika
    じゃあ、いっしょに えいがを みませんか。
    Jaa, issho ni eiga o mimasen ka.
    Then, would you like to watch a movie together?
  • Alex
    いいですね！なんじに あいますか。
    Ii desu ne! Nanji ni aimasu ka.
    Sounds good! What time shall we meet?
  • Mika
    ごご ろくじに えきで あいましょう。
    Gogo rokuji ni eki de aimashou.
    Let's meet at the station at 6 p.m.
  • Alex
    わかりました。たのしみです！
    Wakarimashita. Tanoshimi desu!
    Got it. I'm looking forward to it!
  • Mika
    じゃあ、また あした！
    Jaa, mata ashita!
    See you tomorrow, then!

Grammar

The polite verb ending ます

Polite verbs end in 〜ます: いきます (go), たべます (eat), のみます (drink), みます (watch), あいます (meet). This is the form you'll hear most often when talking to someone you don't know well.

〜ませんか — “Would you like to …?”

Change ます to ませんか to invite someone: えいがを みませんか。 (“Would you like to watch a movie?”). It sounds gentle and gives the other person room to say no.

〜ましょう — “Let's …”

Change ます to ましょう to suggest or agree to something: あいましょう (“Let's meet”). It is the natural answer when a plan is already being made.

に marks a time, で marks where it happens

ろくじに (“at six”) uses に for the time. えきで (“at the station”) uses で for the place where an action happens.

いっしょに — “together”

A small word that makes any invitation friendlier: いっしょに ごはんを たべませんか。

How to say no politely

  • Japanese speakers often avoid a direct “no”. Instead of いいえ you might hear ちょっと… (“That's a bit…”), which means “I'd rather not” or “I can't”. Now you know what it means if someone says it to your invitation.
