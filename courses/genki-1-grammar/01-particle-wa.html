は: The Topic Marker

Goal: By the end you can say who you are and ask about someone with は.

Watch the lesson

The lesson, step by step

Why Japanese needs は

In English every sentence has a subject: “I am a student.” Japanese often works differently. Before it says something, it first tells you what the sentence is about. That thing is called the topic, and the small word that marks it is は.

Think of は as the words “as for”. “As for Mika ... she is a high school student.” First the topic, then the comment about it.

  • ミカさんは こうこうせい です。
    Mika-san wa koukousei desu.
    As for Mika, (she) is a high school student.

The pattern: Topic は Comment

Every beginner sentence in this lesson follows one pattern. The topic comes first. Then は. Then the comment, which says something about the topic. At the very end comes です, which makes the sentence polite.

Everything before は is what we are talking about. Everything after it is what we say about it.

  • わたしは がくせい です。
    Watashi wa gakusei desu.
    As for me, (I am) a student.
  • せんせいは にほんじん です。
    Sensei wa nihonjin desu.
    As for the teacher, (she) is Japanese.
  • アレックスさんは アメリカじん です。
    Arekkusu-san wa Amerikajin desu.
    As for Alex, (he) is American.

Spelling and sound

Here is the part that surprises everyone. The character は is normally read “ha”, as in はな, flower. But when it is the particle, it is pronounced “wa”. You still write は, you just say “wa”.

Only the particle does this. Words that simply contain the character, such as はい for yes, or はじめまして for nice to meet you, keep the “ha” sound.

  • わたしは
    watashi wa
    As for me (the topic)
  • はい
    hai
    yes (here は is just “ha”)
  • はじめまして
    hajimemashite
    nice to meet you (here は is just “ha”)

は does not mean “is”

Many beginners think は means “is”. It does not. The word that works like “is”, “am” and “are” is です. は only marks the topic.

That is why がくせい です on its own already means “(I) am a student”. Add わたしは in front and you make the topic explicit.

  • がくせい です。
    Gakusei desu.
    (I) am a student.
  • わたしは がくせい です。
    Watashi wa gakusei desu.
    As for me, (I) am a student.

You can drop the topic

In English you must say “I”, “she” or “it” every time. Japanese does not. Once the topic is clear, you simply stop saying it. Mika says her name once, and after that she does not need to repeat “as for me” in every sentence.

This is one of the biggest differences between English and Japanese, so get used to hearing sentences without a topic.

  • わたしは ミカ です。
    Watashi wa Mika desu.
    I'm Mika.
  • こうこうせい です。
    Koukousei desu.
    (I'm) a high school student.
  • にほんじん です。
    Nihonjin desu.
    (I'm) Japanese.

Asking back with は

Here is a very useful trick. Say a name, then は, and raise your voice a little. It means “And what about you?” or “How about Alex?”. You do not need a verb or です.

When someone asks you a question, answer it, and then bounce it back with は.

  • ミカさんは？
    Mika-san wa?
    And you, Mika? / What about Mika?
  • せんせいは？
    Sensei wa?
    And the teacher?
Try it: How would you ask Alex, “And you?”

アレックスさんは？

Arekkusu-san wa?: And you, Alex?

は for contrast

Sometimes は does more than mark a topic. It sets one thing against another. If you say “coffee は I drink, tea は I do not drink”, you are contrasting the two. The word order puts the contrasted thing first, with は after it.

You do not need to use this yet. Just notice that は can give a feeling of “as for this one, in particular”.

  • コーヒーは のみます。
    Koohii wa nomimasu.
    Coffee, I drink.
  • おちゃは のみません。
    Ocha wa nomimasen.
    Tea, I don't drink.

Common mistakes

First, do not write the particle with the character わ. It is always spelled with は, even though you say “wa”.

Second, do not add さん to your own name. You say ミカ です, never ミカさん です about yourself.

Third, remember that は marks the topic, not the end of the sentence. Put the topic first, and finish with です.

  • ✗ わたしわ がくせい です。
    (wrong spelling)
    Wrong: the particle is spelled は, not わ.
  • ✗ わたしは ミカさん です。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: do not add さん to your own name.
  • わたしは ミカ です。
    Watashi wa Mika desu.
    Right: I'm Mika.
Try it: Say: “As for Alex, (he) is a university student.”

アレックスさんは だいがくせい です。

Arekkusu-san wa daigakusei desu.: As for Alex, (he) is a university student.

Story: Two new classmates

  • Mika
    わたしは ミカ です。こうこうせい です。
    Watashi wa Mika desu. Koukousei desu.
    I'm Mika. I'm a high school student.
  • Alex
    ぼくは アレックス です。だいがくせい です。
    Boku wa Arekkusu desu. Daigakusei desu.
    I'm Alex. I'm a university student.
  • Mika
    アレックスさんは アメリカじん ですか。
    Arekkusu-san wa Amerikajin desu ka.
    Are you American, Alex?
  • Alex
    はい、アメリカじん です。ミカさんは？
    Hai, Amerikajin desu. Mika-san wa?
    Yes, I'm American. And you, Mika?
  • Mika
    わたしは にほんじん です。
    Watashi wa nihonjin desu.
    I'm Japanese.
  • Alex
    にほんごの せんせいは？
    Nihongo no sensei wa?
    And the Japanese teacher?
  • Mika
    せんせいも にほんじん です。
    Sensei mo nihonjin desu.
    The teacher is Japanese too.

Remember

  • は is read “wa” only when it is a particle. The same character in はい is still “ha”.
  • Do not say さん about yourself. You are ミカ です, never ミカさん です.
