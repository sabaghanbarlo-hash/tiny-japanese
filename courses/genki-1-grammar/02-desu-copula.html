です: The Polite “Is / Am / Are”

Goal: By the end you can make polite “A is B” sentences and you know exactly what です does.

The lesson, step by step

The job of です

です is the Japanese “is”, “am” and “are”. It sits at the end of a sentence and connects the topic to a description. It also makes the sentence polite.

So わたしは がくせい です is “I am a student”, said politely.

  • わたしは がくせい です。
    Watashi wa gakusei desu.
    I am a student.
  • ミカさんは こうこうせい です。
    Mika-san wa koukousei desu.
    Mika is a high school student.

It never changes

In English the verb changes: I am, you are, he is, we are. In Japanese です stays exactly the same, whoever you are talking about, and however many people there are.

That makes it one of the easiest words in the language.

  • わたしは がくせい です。
    Watashi wa gakusei desu.
    I am a student.
  • ミカさんは がくせい です。
    Mika-san wa gakusei desu.
    Mika is a student.
  • わたしたちは がくせい です。
    Watashitachi wa gakusei desu.
    We are students.

です goes last

です goes at the very end. The word before it is the thing you are saying about the topic. If you add the question word か, it comes after です.

So the order is: topic, は, description, です, and then か if you are asking.

  • ミカさんは こうこうせい です。
    Mika-san wa koukousei desu.
    Mika is a high school student.
  • ミカさんは こうこうせい ですか。
    Mika-san wa koukousei desu ka.
    Is Mika a high school student?

Polite and casual

です is the polite style. Between close friends Japanese often uses だ instead, which you will learn in the casual-speech lesson. Courses for beginners start with the polite style, because it is always safe with strangers, teachers and people you have just met.

When in doubt, use です.

  • がくせい です。
    Gakusei desu.
    (I'm) a student. (polite)
  • がくせい だ。
    Gakusei da.
    (I'm) a student. (casual)

です after other words

So far です follows nouns. Later you will see it after adjectives too, as in おいしい です, it is delicious. The job stays the same: it finishes the sentence politely.

Do not worry about that yet. Just notice that です is a general polite ending.

  • おいしい です。
    Oishii desu.
    It's delicious.
  • にほんじん です。
    Nihonjin desu.
    (I'm) Japanese.

そうです and ちがいます

Two phrases you will use all the time. そうです means “that's right” or “that's so”. ちがいます means “that's wrong” or “no, it is not”.

They are short answers to yes and no questions.

  • はい、そうです。
    Hai, sou desu.
    Yes, that's right.
  • いいえ、ちがいます。
    Iie, chigaimasu.
    No, that's wrong.

です is not used with verbs

Do not add です after a verb. Verbs have their own polite ending, ます. たべます is already polite, so たべます です is wrong.

Use です after nouns, and later after adjectives.

  • ✗ たべます です。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: ます verbs do not take です.
  • たべます。
    Tabemasu.
    Right: I eat.

Common mistakes

Do not put です in the middle of a sentence, and do not add さん to your own name before です. You say ミカ です, not ミカさん です.

Remember that は marks the topic and です ends the sentence. They are two different jobs.

  • ✗ わたしは です がくせい。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: です must come last.
  • ✗ わたしは ミカさん です。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: no さん for yourself.
  • わたしは ミカ です。
    Watashi wa Mika desu.
    Right: I'm Mika.
Try it: Say: “Mika is a teacher.”

ミカさんは せんせい です。

Mika-san wa sensei desu.: Mika is a teacher.

Story: The first day of class

  • Mika
    おはよう ございます。わたしは ミカ です。
    Ohayou gozaimasu. Watashi wa Mika desu.
    Good morning. I'm Mika.
  • Alex
    おはよう ございます。アレックス です。
    Ohayou gozaimasu. Arekkusu desu.
    Good morning. I'm Alex.
  • Mika
    アレックスさんは だいがくせい ですか。
    Arekkusu-san wa daigakusei desu ka.
    Are you a university student, Alex?
  • Alex
    はい、そうです。ミカさんは？
    Hai, sou desu. Mika-san wa?
    Yes, that's right. And you, Mika?
  • Mika
    わたしは こうこうせい です。
    Watashi wa koukousei desu.
    I'm a high school student.
  • Alex
    あの ひとは せんせい ですか。
    Ano hito wa sensei desu ka.
    Is that person the teacher?
  • Mika
    いいえ、ちがいます。がくせい です。
    Iie, chigaimasu. Gakusei desu.
    No, that's wrong. (He's) a student.

Remember

  • です never changes: it is the same for I, you, he, she, we and they.
  • です finishes polite sentences about nouns. Verbs use ます instead.
  • Say ミカ です about yourself, never ミカさん です.
