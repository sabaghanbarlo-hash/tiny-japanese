か: The Question Particle

Goal: By the end you can ask yes/no questions and answer them.

Watch the lesson

The lesson, step by step

How Japanese asks questions

In English you rearrange the sentence: “You are a student” becomes “Are you a student?” Japanese does not rearrange anything. You keep the same sentence and add one small word at the end: か. That is the whole trick.

So once you can make a polite statement, you can already make a question.

  • がくせい です。
    Gakusei desu.
    (I'm) a student.
  • がくせい ですか。
    Gakusei desu ka.
    Are (you) a student?

From statement to question

Take any polite sentence that ends in です or ます. Put か after it and it becomes a question. Japanese writing normally ends with a full stop even for questions, and a question mark is optional.

Nothing else in the sentence changes: not the word order, not the particles.

  • アレックスさんは がくせい です。
    Arekkusu-san wa gakusei desu.
    Alex is a student.
  • アレックスさんは がくせい ですか。
    Arekkusu-san wa gakusei desu ka.
    Is Alex a student?
  • ミカさんは にほんじん ですか。
    Mika-san wa nihonjin desu ka.
    Is Mika Japanese?

Your voice

When you speak, your voice rises a little on か. In polite speech the rise is gentle. Do not overdo it, because か already tells the listener that this is a question.

If you say a sentence without か and with a strongly rising voice, it can also be a question, but that is casual speech. For now, always add か.

  • アメリカじん ですか。
    Amerikajin desu ka.
    Are you American?

Answering yes or no

Answer yes with はい and no with いいえ. Then repeat the key part of the sentence. You can also answer with そうです, which means “that's right”.

For a polite “no, that is wrong”, you can say いいえ、ちがいます.

  • はい、がくせい です。
    Hai, gakusei desu.
    Yes, (I'm) a student.
  • はい、そうです。
    Hai, sou desu.
    Yes, that's right.
  • いいえ、ちがいます。
    Iie, chigaimasu.
    No, that's wrong.

Question words

Question words already make the sentence a question, and you still put か at the end. なに is what, だれ is who, and どこ is where.

The question word goes where the answer will go. In “Who is that person?”, だれ sits in the spot where a name would be.

  • あの ひとは だれ ですか。
    Ano hito wa dare desu ka.
    Who is that person?
  • としょかんは どこ ですか。
    Toshokan wa doko desu ka.
    Where is the library?
  • これは なん ですか。
    Kore wa nan desu ka.
    What is this?

なに or なん?

なに changes to なん before です. So you say なん ですか, not なに ですか. The same change happens before words that start with the sounds t, d and n.

Before other particles, such as を, it stays なに.

  • これは なん ですか。
    Kore wa nan desu ka.
    What is this?
  • なにを たべますか。
    Nani o tabemasu ka.
    What do you eat?

そうですか

You will hear そうですか constantly. When you say it with a falling voice, it does not ask anything. It means “Oh, I see” or “Is that so?”, and it is the natural way to react to news.

With a rising voice, it really does ask “Is that so?”. Listen to Mika and Alex to hear the difference.

  • そうですか。
    Sou desu ka.
    Oh, I see. / Is that so?
  • はい、そうです。
    Hai, sou desu.
    Yes, that's right.

Common mistakes

Do not put か in the middle of a sentence, and do not use it to end a statement. か always comes last, and it turns the sentence into a question.

Remember also that なに becomes なん before です.

  • ✗ アレックスさんか がくせい です。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: か must be at the end.
  • アレックスさんは がくせい ですか。
    Arekkusu-san wa gakusei desu ka.
    Right: Is Alex a student?
Try it: Ask: “Is Mika Japanese?”

ミカさんは にほんじん ですか。

Mika-san wa nihonjin desu ka.: Is Mika Japanese?

Vocabulary

  • なに / なんnani / nanwhat (なん before です)
  • だれdarewho
  • たこやきtakoyakitakoyaki, octopus balls
  • りょうりryouridish, cooking
  • みせmiseshop
  • おきゃくさんokyakusancustomer
  • ちがいますchigaimasuthat's wrong, no

Story: At the school festival

  • Alex
    すみません。これは なん ですか。
    Sumimasen. Kore wa nan desu ka.
    Excuse me. What is this?
  • Mika
    それは たこやき です。
    Sore wa takoyaki desu.
    That's takoyaki.
  • Alex
    たこやきは にほんの りょうり ですか。
    Takoyaki wa nihon no ryouri desu ka.
    Is takoyaki a Japanese dish?
  • Mika
    はい、そうです。
    Hai, sou desu.
    Yes, it is.
  • Alex
    あの ひとは だれ ですか。
    Ano hito wa dare desu ka.
    Who is that person?
  • Mika
    あの ひとは わたしの せんせい です。
    Ano hito wa watashi no sensei desu.
    That person is my teacher.
  • Alex
    せんせいは たこやきの みせの ひと ですか。
    Sensei wa takoyaki no mise no hito desu ka.
    Is your teacher a person from the takoyaki shop?
  • Mika
    いいえ、ちがいます。おきゃくさん です。
    Iie, chigaimasu. Okyakusan desu.
    No, not at all. A customer.

Remember

  • Say it with a slightly rising voice on か, and you will sound natural.
  • なん is used before です and before words starting with t, d or n; otherwise you say なに.
