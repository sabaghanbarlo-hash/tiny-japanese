の: Linking Nouns

Goal: By the end you can join two nouns with の in the right order.

Watch the lesson

The lesson, step by step

What の does

の links two nouns. The first noun tells you more about the second one. It can mean “of”, or the possessive “'s”. わたしの is “my”, literally “of me”.

It is one of the most common words in Japanese, so it is worth understanding properly.

  • わたしの かばん
    watashi no kaban
    my bag
  • ミカさんの かさ
    Mika-san no kasa
    Mika's umbrella

Owner の thing

The most common use is ownership. The owner comes first, then の, then the thing. It is the same order as the English apostrophe s: owner first, thing second.

Mika's umbrella is ミカさんの かさ: Mika, の, umbrella.

  • わたしの かばん
    watashi no kaban
    my bag
  • ミカさんの かさ
    Mika-san no kasa
    Mika's umbrella
  • せんせいの ほん
    sensei no hon
    the teacher's book

Describing with の

の also tells you what kind of thing something is. A book about Japanese becomes a Japanese book. A teacher of the university becomes a university teacher.

Here the first noun does not own the second. It narrows it down.

  • にほんごの ほん
    nihongo no hon
    a Japanese book
  • だいがくの せんせい
    daigaku no sensei
    a university teacher
  • にほんの りょうり
    Nihon no ryouri
    Japanese food

The order is reversed

Watch the order. In English, “teacher of Japanese” puts the main word first. In Japanese the main word is always last. にほんごの せんせい is literally “Japanese's teacher”.

Change the order and you change the meaning. せんせいの にほんご is “the teacher's Japanese”.

  • にほんごの せんせい
    nihongo no sensei
    a Japanese teacher
  • せんせいの にほんご
    sensei no nihongo
    the teacher's Japanese

Chains of の

You can chain more than two nouns. Read from the left, and the last noun is always the main thing. わたしの ともだちの かばん is my friend's bag. The bag is the main thing.

Each の adds one more detail in front of the final noun.

  • わたしの ともだちの かばん
    watashi no tomodachi no kaban
    my friend's bag
  • にほんごの せんせいの なまえ
    nihongo no sensei no namae
    the Japanese teacher's name

Whose? だれの

Put だれ, who, in front of の and you get だれの: whose. It goes right before the thing you are asking about.

The answer replaces だれの with a name and の, or with わたしの.

  • これは だれの かばん ですか。
    Kore wa dare no kaban desu ka.
    Whose bag is this?
  • わたしの かばん です。
    Watashi no kaban desu.
    It's my bag.

の and は are different

Do not mix up の and は. は marks the topic of a whole sentence. の lives inside a noun phrase and joins two nouns.

ミカさんは せんせい です says Mika is a teacher. ミカさんの せんせい says Mika's teacher.

  • ミカさんは せんせい です。
    Mika-san wa sensei desu.
    Mika is a teacher.
  • ミカさんの せんせい
    Mika-san no sensei
    Mika's teacher

Common mistakes

Do not reverse the order: the owner comes first. And do not use は where you mean “my” or “Mika's”. は would make a sentence, and a noun phrase like “my bag” is not a sentence.

If you are unsure, ask yourself: which noun is the main thing? Put it last.

  • ✗ かばんの わたし
    (wrong)
    Wrong: this means “the me of the bag”.
  • ✗ わたしは かばん
    (wrong)
    Wrong: は does not mean “my”.
  • わたしの かばん
    watashi no kaban
    Right: my bag.
Try it: Say: “my teacher's name”

わたしの せんせいの なまえ

watashi no sensei no namae: my teacher's name

    It's a Japanese book, isn't it.

Remember

  • Read のs from the left. わたしの ともだちの かばん is “my friend's bag”.
  • の links two nouns. It does not link a noun to a verb.
