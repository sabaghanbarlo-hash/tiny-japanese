これ, それ, あれ: This and That

Goal: By the end you can point at things, ask “what is this?” and say where things are.

The lesson, step by step

The idea: distance

Japanese has three words for “this” and “that”, and the difference is distance. これ is near me. それ is near you. あれ is far from both of us.

So it is not only about how far something is. It is about how far it is from the speaker and the listener.

  • これは ほん です。
    Kore wa hon desu.
    This is a book. (near me)
  • それは かばん です。
    Sore wa kaban desu.
    That is a bag. (near you)
  • あれは がっこう です。
    Are wa gakkou desu.
    That over there is a school.

The whole family

These words come in families that start with こ, そ, あ and ど. ど always means “which?”. The things family is これ, それ, あれ, どれ.

The same pattern gives you この and ここ. Once you know the four starting sounds, you know all the families.

  • これ
    kore
    this one
  • それ
    sore
    that one (near you)
  • あれ
    are
    that one over there
  • どれ
    dore
    which one?

これ, それ, あれ stand alone

これ, それ and あれ take the place of a noun. They stand alone, like “this” in English. You cannot put a noun straight after them.

To ask what something is, use なん: これは なん ですか.

  • これは なん ですか。
    Kore wa nan desu ka.
    What is this?
  • それは たこやき です。
    Sore wa takoyaki desu.
    That is takoyaki.

この, その, あの before a noun

When you want “this book” or “that person”, use この, その and あの directly before the noun. They never stand alone.

So この ほん is “this book”, and あの ひと is “that person over there”.

  • この ほん
    kono hon
    this book
  • その かばん
    sono kaban
    that bag (near you)
  • あの ひとは せんせい です。
    Ano hito wa sensei desu.
    That person over there is a teacher.

Places: ここ, そこ, あそこ

The same pattern names places. ここ is here, そこ is there near you, あそこ is over there, and どこ is where?

These are useful for asking where things are, and for pointing someone to the toilet, the station or the exit.

  • ここは がっこう です。
    Koko wa gakkou desu.
    This is a school. (here)
  • トイレは あそこ です。
    Toire wa asoko desu.
    The toilet is over there.
  • としょかんは どこ ですか。
    Toshokan wa doko desu ka.
    Where is the library?

Asking which: どれ and どの

When there are three or more things, ask どれ for “which one?”. Before a noun, use どの: どの ほん is “which book?”.

The answer uses これ, それ or あれ, or the この family.

  • ミカさんの かばんは どれ ですか。
    Mika-san no kaban wa dore desu ka.
    Which one is Mika's bag?
  • どの ひとが せんせい ですか。
    Dono hito ga sensei desu ka.
    Which person is the teacher?

Common mistakes

Do not put a noun after これ, それ or あれ. Use この, その, あの there. And do not leave この alone without a noun.

A simple rule: これ replaces the noun, この describes it.

  • ✗ これ ほんは ミカさんの です。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: use この ほん for “this book”.
  • ✗ この は ほん です。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: この needs a noun after it.
  • この ほんは ミカさんの ほん です。
    Kono hon wa Mika-san no hon desu.
    Right: This book is Mika's book.
Try it: Say: “That (over there) is a school.”

あれは がっこう です。

Are wa gakkou desu.: That over there is a school.

Remember

  • Ko is near me, so is near you, a is far from both, and do means which.
  • これ stands alone; この always needs a noun after it.
  • ここ, そこ, あそこ and どこ are places, and they follow the same four sounds.
