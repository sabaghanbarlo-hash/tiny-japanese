ね: Looking for Agreement

Goal: By the end you can check information and sound friendly with ね.

Watch the lesson

Turn your sound on: listen to each sound, then say it out loud yourself. Tap any character or speaker button on this page to hear it again. Voices: Mika and Alex (neural text-to-speech); teacher narration: neural text-to-speech.

The lesson, step by step

What ね does

ね goes at the end of a sentence. It asks the listener to agree, or it shares a feeling. It is like “right?”, “isn't it?” or “huh”.

It makes you sound friendly, because you are inviting the listener to share your view.

  • ミカさんは こうこうせい ですね。
    Mika-san wa koukousei desu ne.
    You're a high school student, aren't you?

Two uses

There are two main uses. When your voice rises, ね checks information: “you are a student, right?”. When your voice falls, ね shares a feeling: “that's good, isn't it”, expecting a nod.

In both cases, you expect the listener to say はい or agree.

  • そうですね。
    Sou desu ne.
    That's right. / Indeed.
  • ミカさんは こうこうせい ですね。
    Mika-san wa koukousei desu ne.
    You're a high school student, aren't you? (checking)

Where ね goes

Put ね at the end, after です or ます. It comes in the same place as か, but it does not turn the sentence into a question for new information.

You can also put ね after short phrases, but for now, keep it at the end.

  • あした いきますね。
    Ashita ikimasu ne.
    You're going tomorrow, aren't you?
  • にほんじん ですね。
    Nihonjin desu ne.
    (You're) Japanese, aren't you?

ね sounds friendly

Compare がくせい です and がくせい ですね. The first is a plain statement. The second invites the listener to agree, and it feels warmer.

Japanese speakers add ね constantly, to keep the conversation smooth.

  • がくせい です。
    Gakusei desu.
    (I'm) a student.
  • がくせい ですね。
    Gakusei desu ne.
    (You're) a student, aren't you?

そうですね

そうですね means “that's right” or “indeed”. It also works as “let me see”, when you are thinking about your answer. It is one of the most common replies in Japanese.

Say it with a falling voice when you agree.

  • はい、そうですね。
    Hai, sou desu ne.
    Yes, that's right.
  • そうですね。
    Sou desu ne.
    Let me see. / That's so.

ね or か?

か asks a real question: you do not know the answer. ね asks for agreement: you think you know the answer and want confirmation.

ミカさんは がくせい ですか asks “Is Mika a student?”. ミカさんは がくせい ですね says “Mika is a student, isn't she?”.

  • ミカさんは がくせい ですか。
    Mika-san wa gakusei desu ka.
    Is Mika a student? (I don't know)
  • ミカさんは がくせい ですね。
    Mika-san wa gakusei desu ne.
    Mika is a student, isn't she? (I think so)
Try it: Say: “You're Japanese, aren't you?”

にほんじん ですね。

Nihonjin desu ne.: You're Japanese, aren't you?

  • いい ひとii hitoa nice person
  • ほんとうにhontou nireally

Remember

  • ね asks for agreement; か asks for new information.
  • Rising ね checks; falling ね shares a feeling.
  • Adding ね makes you sound warmer and more natural.
