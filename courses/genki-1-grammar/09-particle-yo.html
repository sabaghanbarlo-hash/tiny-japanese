よ: Telling Something New

Goal: By the end you can share information with よ and combine it as よね.

Watch the lesson

The lesson, step by step

What よ does

よ goes at the end of a sentence. It tells the listener something they may not know, or gives emphasis. It is like “you know” or “I'm telling you”.

ミカさんは にほんじん ですよ means “Mika is Japanese, you know”.

  • ミカさんは にほんじん ですよ。
    Mika-san wa nihonjin desu yo.
    Mika is Japanese, you know.

ね vs よ

The difference is who knows. ね says “we both know this, right?”. よ says “I know this, and I think you may not”.

Use ね to agree, and よ to inform.

  • にほんじん ですね。
    Nihonjin desu ne.
    (You're) Japanese, aren't you? (agree)
  • にほんじん ですよ。
    Nihonjin desu yo.
    (He's) Japanese, you know. (inform)

Friendly notices

よ is great for friendly notices: “there's a test tomorrow, you know”, “this is the toilet, you know”. You are telling someone something you think they would want to know.

It keeps the tone light and helpful.

  • あした テストが ありますよ。
    Ashita tesuto ga arimasu yo.
    There's a test tomorrow, you know.
  • トイレは あそこ ですよ。
    Toire wa asoko desu yo.
    The toilet is over there, you know.

Do not overuse よ

よ puts weight on your information. Used too often, or with information the listener should already know, it can sound pushy, or like you are lecturing.

With a teacher or someone older, use it carefully, and soften with a polite tone.

  • ここは がっこう ですよ。
    Koko wa gakkou desu yo.
    This is a school, you know. (could sound pushy)

よね

You can combine them: よね. It means “it is so, right?”: you believe something and ask for confirmation, a little more strongly than ね alone.

にほんじん ですよね: “(You're) Japanese, right?”.

  • にほんじん ですよね。
    Nihonjin desu yo ne.
    (You're) Japanese, right?
  • はい、そうです。
    Hai, sou desu.
    Yes, that's right.
Try it: Say: “The toilet is over there, you know.”

トイレは あそこ ですよ。

Toire wa asoko desu yo.: The toilet is over there, you know.

Story: News before class

  • Mika
    アレックスさん、あした テストが ありますよ。
    Arekkusu-san, ashita tesuto ga arimasu yo.
    Alex, there's a test tomorrow, you know.
  • Alex
    えっ、そうですか。しりませんでした。
    E, sou desu ka. Shirimasen deshita.
    Oh, is that so? I didn't know.
  • Mika
    ひるごはんの あとに ありますよ。
    Hirugohan no ato ni arimasu yo.
    It's after lunch, you know.
  • Alex
    ありがとう。ミカさんは しんせつ ですね。
    Arigatou. Mika-san wa shinsetsu desu ne.
    Thanks. You're kind, aren't you, Mika?
  • Mika
    いいえ、ともだち ですよ。
    Iie, tomodachi desu yo.
    No, we're friends, you know.

Remember

  • よ gives new information; ね asks for agreement.
  • Too many よ can sound pushy, so use it for helpful notices.
  • よね means “right?”, said with some confidence.
