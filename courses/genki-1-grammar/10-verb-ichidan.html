Verbs, Part 1: Ichidan (る) Verbs

Goal: By the end you can recognise an ichidan verb and say which sound comes before る.

Watch the lesson

The lesson, step by step

What a Japanese verb looks like

In English a verb is “eat”, “see”, “sleep”. In Japanese the dictionary form of every verb ends in a u-sound: く, む, す, る and so on. That is how you find a verb in the dictionary.

Japanese verbs fall into three groups. In this lesson you meet the first group, the ichidan verbs.

  • たべる
    taberu
    to eat
  • みる
    miru
    to see, to watch
  • ねる
    neru
    to sleep

The name: ichidan

いちだん means “one step”. It is called that because, when the verb changes its ending, you only drop one sound and add something new. The group is also called る-verbs, because every ichidan verb ends in る.

Do not worry about the changes yet. First, learn to recognise the group.

  • たべる
    taberu
    to eat
  • みる
    miru
    to see, to watch
  • おきる
    okiru
    to wake up, to get up
  • ねる
    neru
    to sleep

The rule

A verb is ichidan when it ends in る and the sound just before る is an i-sound or an e-sound. Look at the syllable before the last one. In たべる, it is べ, an e-sound. In みる, it is み, an i-sound.

So る after い or え sounds is the sign of an ichidan verb.

  • たべる
    ta-be-ru: be = e-sound
    to eat
  • おきる
    o-ki-ru: ki = i-sound
    to wake up
  • ねる
    ne-ru: ne = e-sound
    to sleep
  • みる
    mi-ru: mi = i-sound
    to see

More ichidan verbs

Start with a few very common ichidan verbs: たべる, eat. みる, see or watch. おきる, wake up. ねる, sleep. でる, go out or leave.

You will use these in the polite form in the next lesson.

  • でる
    deru
    to go out, to leave
  • おきる
    okiru
    to wake up
  • ねる
    neru
    to sleep

Not every る verb is ichidan

A few verbs end in る with an i-sound or an e-sound before it, but they belong to the other group. The ones you must learn first are かえる, to return, はいる, to enter, and しる, to know.

They are godan verbs in disguise. You will meet that group in the next lesson. For now, remember these three as exceptions.

  • かえる
    kaeru
    to return (exception: godan)
  • はいる
    hairu
    to enter (exception: godan)
  • しる
    shiru
    to know (exception: godan)

Dictionary form is plain

The dictionary form is the plain, casual form of the verb. With friends you can say ごはんを たべる, “I eat”. You will study casual speech later. The polite ます form, the one you use with teachers and strangers, is made from this dictionary form.

That is why recognising the group first is so useful.

  • ごはんを たべる。
    Gohan o taberu.
    (I) eat a meal. (casual)
  • テレビを みる。
    Terebi o miru.
    (I) watch TV. (casual)

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is to think every verb ending in る is ichidan. Always check the sound before る. An a-sound, u-sound or o-sound means it is not ichidan, as in のる, to ride, and わかる, to understand.

And remember the exceptions: かえる, はいる and しる.

  • のる
    noru
    to ride: o + る, so not ichidan
  • わかる
    wakaru
    to understand: ka + る, so not ichidan
  • かえる
    kaeru
    to return: looks ichidan, but is godan

Story: Which group is it?

  • Alex
    たべる は いちだんどうし ですか。
    Taberu wa ichidan doushi desu ka.
    Is taberu an ichidan verb?
  • Mika
    はい、そうです。「べ」の あとに「る」です。
    Hai, sou desu. “Be” no ato ni “ru” desu.
    Yes, it is. It's “ru” after “be”.
  • Alex
    みる も いちだんどうし ですか。
    Miru mo ichidan doushi desu ka.
    Is miru an ichidan verb too?
  • Mika
    はい。「み」の あとに「る」です。
    Hai. “Mi” no ato ni “ru” desu.
    Yes. It's “ru” after “mi”.
  • Alex
    かえる は？
    Kaeru wa?
    What about kaeru?
  • Mika
    かえる は れいがい です。ごだんどうし です。
    Kaeru wa reigai desu. Godan doushi desu.
    Kaeru is an exception. It's a godan verb.
  • Alex
    えっ、そうですか。むずかしい ですね。
    E, sou desu ka. Muzukashii desu ne.
    Oh, is that so? That's difficult.

Remember

  • Ichidan verbs end in る, with an i-sound or an e-sound before it.
  • かえる, はいる and しる look ichidan but are godan. Learn them as exceptions.
  • The dictionary form is plain and casual; the polite ます form comes from it.
