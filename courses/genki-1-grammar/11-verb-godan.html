Verbs, Part 2: Godan (う) Verbs

Goal: By the end you can sort a verb into ichidan or godan.

The lesson, step by step

The second group

ごだん means “five steps”. It is the biggest group of verbs. Its dictionary forms can end in any u-sound: う, く, ぐ, す, つ, ぬ, ぶ, む or る.

It is also called う-verbs, because the ending is a u-sound.

  • のむ
    nomu
    to drink
  • いく
    iku
    to go
  • はなす
    hanasu
    to speak, to talk
  • かう
    kau
    to buy

Why five steps?

In this group the last sound changes along the five vowel rows: a, i, u, e, o. For example, のむ can become のまない, のみます, のむ, のめば and のもう. Five steps.

You will meet these forms one by one. The most important one now is the い-step: のみます.

  • のむ → のみます
    nomu → nomimasu
    to drink → drink (polite)

The endings

A godan verb can end in nine different sounds. Here are common examples: かう, buy. かく, write. およぐ, swim. はなす, speak. まつ, wait. あそぶ, play. のむ, drink. のる, ride.

The ending ぬ is very rare. The only common verb with it is しぬ, to die, so we skip it.

  • かう
    kau
    to buy
  • かく
    kaku
    to write
  • およぐ
    oyogu
    to swim
  • はなす
    hanasu
    to speak
  • まつ
    matsu
    to wait
  • あそぶ
    asobu
    to play
  • のむ
    nomu
    to drink
  • のる
    noru
    to ride

る verbs in this group

Godan verbs can also end in る. You tell them from ichidan verbs by the sound before る. If it is あ, う or お, the verb is godan: わかる, understand. つくる, make. のる, ride.

Only i-sound and e-sound plus る could be ichidan.

  • わかる
    wakaru
    to understand
  • つくる
    tsukuru
    to make
  • のる
    noru
    to ride

The exceptions

Remember the exceptions from the last lesson. These godan verbs end in る with an i-sound or an e-sound before it: かえる, return. はいる, enter. しる, know. はしる, run.

Treat them as vocabulary you simply have to remember.

  • かえる
    kaeru
    to return
  • はいる
    hairu
    to enter
  • しる
    shiru
    to know
  • はしる
    hashiru
    to run

A quick test

First look at the ending. If the verb does not end in る, it is godan. If it ends in る, look before it. A, u or o means godan. I or e usually means ichidan, unless it is one of the exceptions.

That is all you need for now. The more verbs you meet, the faster it becomes automatic.

  • のむ
    nomu
    ends in む: godan
  • たべる
    taberu
    e + る: ichidan
  • のる
    noru
    o + る: godan
  • かえる
    kaeru
    exception: godan

Story: Weekend plans

  • Mika
    しゅうまつ、なにを しますか。
    Shuumatsu, nani o shimasu ka.
    What are you doing this weekend?
  • Alex
    ともだちと バスに のります。
    Tomodachi to basu ni norimasu.
    I'm riding the bus with a friend.
  • Mika
    そして なにを かいますか。
    Soshite nani o kaimasu ka.
    And what will you buy?
  • Alex
    ほんを かいます。それから コーヒーを のみます。
    Hon o kaimasu. Sorekara koohii o nomimasu.
    I'll buy a book. After that I'll drink coffee.
  • Mika
    いいですね。わたしは うちに かえります。
    Ii desu ne. Watashi wa uchi ni kaerimasu.
    Sounds nice. I'm going home.
  • Alex
    そして にほんごを べんきょうします。
    Soshite nihongo o benkyou shimasu.
    And I'll study Japanese.

Remember

  • Godan verbs can end in う, く, ぐ, す, つ, ぬ, ぶ, む or る.
  • る after a, u or o means godan. る after i or e usually means ichidan.
  • Remember the exceptions: かえる, はいる, しる and はしる.
