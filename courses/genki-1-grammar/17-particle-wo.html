を: The Object Marker

Goal: By the end you can say what you eat, drink, read and watch.

Watch the lesson

Turn your sound on: listen to each sound, then say it out loud yourself. Tap any character or speaker button on this page to hear it again. Voices: Mika and Alex (neural text-to-speech); teacher narration: neural text-to-speech.

The lesson, step by step

The idea: what the action is done to

を marks the object of a verb: the thing the action is done to. If you eat bread, bread is the object. In English you recognise the object by its position after the verb. Japanese marks it with を, so you always know which word it is.

Whenever you see を, the word right before it is the thing being eaten, drunk, read, watched or bought.

  • パンを たべます。
    Pan o tabemasu.
    I eat bread.
  • ほんを よみます。
    Hon o yomimasu.
    I read a book.

Spelling and sound

を sounds exactly like お. The difference is only in the writing: を is used only as a particle. You will never find it inside an ordinary word.

So if you see を in a sentence, you know a particle is there and the word before it is an object.

  • コーヒーを のみます。
    Koohii o nomimasu.
    I drink coffee.
  • おちゃを のみます。
    Ocha o nomimasu.
    I drink tea.

The pattern: Object を verb

The pattern is: object, を, verb. The verb is always last. わたしは パンを たべます is literally “as for me, bread eat”.

Notice that は and を do different jobs. は marks the topic, usually the person. を marks the thing.

  • わたしは パンを たべます。
    Watashi wa pan o tabemasu.
    I eat bread.
  • ミカさんは おちゃを のみます。
    Mika-san wa ocha o nomimasu.
    Mika drinks tea.
  • アレックスさんは しんぶんを よみます。
    Arekkusu-san wa shinbun o yomimasu.
    Alex reads the newspaper.

Four verbs to start with

The ending ます makes a verb polite. たべます is eat, のみます is drink, みます is see or watch, and よみます is read.

Learn each verb together with a typical object. That is how Japanese sticks in your memory.

  • ごはんを たべます。
    Gohan o tabemasu.
    I eat rice (a meal).
  • みずを のみます。
    Mizu o nomimasu.
    I drink water.
  • テレビを みます。
    Terebi o mimasu.
    I watch TV.
  • ほんを よみます。
    Hon o yomimasu.
    I read a book.

Which verbs take を?

Verbs that do something to something take を: eat, drink, read, watch, buy, make. But some verbs do not act on an object, like いきます, go, or あいます, meet. You cannot “go something”.

Those verbs use other particles, such as に. You already learned that に marks the place you go to.

  • ほんを かいます。
    Hon o kaimasu.
    I buy a book.
  • がっこうに いきます。
    Gakkou ni ikimasu.
    I go to school. (no を)

Asking: なにを

To ask what someone eats, put なに before を: なにを たべますか. The answer simply replaces なに with the real object.

This is the most useful question for any conversation about daily life.

  • なにを たべますか。
    Nani o tabemasu ka.
    What do you eat?
  • パンを たべます。
    Pan o tabemasu.
    I eat bread.

は and を together

A sentence can have both. は marks the topic, usually the person doing the action, and を marks the object. Each particle has its own job, and neither replaces the other.

When the topic is clear, you can drop it and keep the object and the verb: パンを たべます.

  • アレックスさんは コーヒーを のみます。
    Arekkusu-san wa koohii o nomimasu.
    Alex drinks coffee.
  • コーヒーを のみます。
    Koohii o nomimasu.
    (I) drink coffee.

Common mistakes

Keep the verb at the end of the sentence. And remember that あいます takes に, not を: ともだちに あいます.

If you are unsure, ask: is this word the thing the verb is done to? If yes, it takes を.

  • ✗ たべます パンを。
    (wrong)
    Wrong for now: the verb goes last.
  • ✗ ともだちを あいます。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: あいます takes に.
  • パンを たべます。
    Pan o tabemasu.
    Right: I eat bread.
Try it: Say: “I read a newspaper.”

しんぶんを よみます。

Shinbun o yomimasu.: I read a newspaper.

Story: What do you have in the morning?

  • Mika
    アレックスさん、あさ なにを たべますか。
    Arekkusu-san, asa nani o tabemasu ka.
    Alex, what do you eat in the morning?
  • Alex
    パンを たべます。そして コーヒーを のみます。
    Pan o tabemasu. Soshite koohii o nomimasu.
    I eat bread. And I drink coffee.
  • Mika
    わたしは ごはんを たべます。おちゃを のみます。
    Watashi wa gohan o tabemasu. Ocha o nomimasu.
    I eat rice. I drink tea.
  • Alex
    あさ テレビを みますか。
    Asa terebi o mimasu ka.
    Do you watch TV in the morning?
  • Mika
    はい、ニュースを みます。
    Hai, nyuusu o mimasu.
    Yes, I watch the news.
  • Alex
    わたしは しんぶんを よみます。
    Watashi wa shinbun o yomimasu.
    I read the newspaper.

Remember

  • を is read “o”, and it only ever appears as a particle.
  • The verb comes last. Thing を verb, never verb を thing.
