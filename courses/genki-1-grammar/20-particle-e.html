へ: Direction

Goal: By the end you can say where you go, come or return to.

The lesson, step by step

The idea: direction

へ marks the direction you are heading: the place a movement is aimed at. Think of an arrow pointing toward a place, while に marks the dot where you arrive.

In everyday beginner Japanese the two overlap a lot, so this lesson is about knowing what へ can and cannot do.

  • きょうとへ いきます。
    Kyouto e ikimasu.
    I go to Kyoto.

Spelling and sound

Just like は, this particle is spelled one way and said another way. The character へ is read “he” in ordinary words, such as へや, room. But as the particle, it is pronounced “e”.

You write へ, you say “e”. If you see へ after a place and before a verb of movement, read it “e”.

  • きょうとへ
    Kyouto e
    toward Kyoto
  • うちへ
    uchi e
    toward home
  • へや
    heya
    room (here へ is just “he”)

With verbs of movement

Use へ with the verbs of movement: いきます, go, きます, come, and かえります, return. The place is where you are heading.

These three verbs are the main partners of へ.

  • がっこうへ いきます。
    Gakkou e ikimasu.
    I go to school.
  • にほんへ きます。
    Nihon e kimasu.
    I come to Japan.
  • うちへ かえります。
    Uchi e kaerimasu.
    I go home.

へ and に

With verbs of movement, へ and に are almost the same. がっこうへ いきます and がっこうに いきます both mean “I go to school”.

へ stresses the direction, and に stresses the arrival point. As a beginner you can use either one.

  • がっこうへ いきます。
    Gakkou e ikimasu.
    I head to school.
  • がっこうに いきます。
    Gakkou ni ikimasu.
    I go to school.

What へ cannot do

へ only works for directions. It cannot mark a time, so you cannot say しちじへ おきます. And it cannot mark the person you meet, so ともだちへ あいます is wrong.

For times and people, use に.

  • ✗ しちじへ おきます。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: times take に.
  • ✗ ともだちへ あいます。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: people take に.
  • しちじに おきます。
    Shichiji ni okimasu.
    Right: I get up at seven.

Asking: どこへ

Ask どこへ いきますか: where are you going? どこに works too. The answer replaces どこ with a place.

Add a time word like あした, tomorrow, at the start to say when.

  • どこへ いきますか。
    Doko e ikimasu ka.
    Where are you going?
  • きょうとへ いきます。
    Kyouto e ikimasu.
    I'm going to Kyoto.
  • あした きょうとへ いきます。
    Ashita Kyouto e ikimasu.
    I'm going to Kyoto tomorrow.

Common mistakes

Remember that going is not an action done to a place. You do not say きょうとを いきます. The place takes へ or に.

And remember that the verb goes last.

  • ✗ きょうとを いきます。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: use へ or に for the destination.
  • きょうとへ いきます。
    Kyouto e ikimasu.
    Right: I go to Kyoto.
Try it: Say: “I go home by train.”

でんしゃで うちへ かえります。

Densha de uchi e kaerimasu.: I go home by train.

Remember

  • へ is read “e” as a particle.
  • For times use に, and for directions use へ or に.
