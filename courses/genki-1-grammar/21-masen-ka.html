ませんか: Inviting Someone

Goal: By the end you can invite someone and answer an invitation.

Watch the lesson

Turn your sound on: listen to each sound, then say it out loud yourself. Tap any character or speaker button on this page to hear it again. Voices: Mika and Alex (neural text-to-speech); teacher narration: neural text-to-speech.

The lesson, step by step

The idea

ませんか looks like a negative question: “Do you not ...?” But in Japanese it works as an invitation, like “Would you like to ...?” or “Shall we ...?”.

コーヒーを のみませんか means “Would you like to drink coffee?”.

  • コーヒーを のみませんか。
    Koohii o nomimasen ka.
    Would you like to drink coffee?

The pattern

Make the ます form negative, and add か. いきます becomes いきませんか. みます becomes みませんか.

It is a polite and gentle way to suggest something.

  • あした えいがを みませんか。
    Ashita eiga o mimasen ka.
    Would you like to watch a movie tomorrow?
  • いっしょに いきませんか。
    Issho ni ikimasen ka.
    Shall we go together?

いっしょに

いっしょに means “together”. It often appears in invitations, to make it clear that you want to do the activity with the other person.

It goes before the verb.

  • いっしょに たべませんか。
    Issho ni tabemasen ka.
    Shall we eat together?
  • いっしょに べんきょうしませんか。
    Issho ni benkyou shimasen ka.
    Shall we study together?

Accepting

To accept, say はい、いいですね, “yes, that sounds good”. You can also say はい、ぜひ, “yes, by all means”.

Be warm: an invitation is a friendly gesture.

  • はい、いいですね。
    Hai, ii desu ne.
    Yes, that sounds good.
  • はい、ぜひ。
    Hai, zehi.
    Yes, by all means.

Declining politely

Japanese avoids a blunt “no”. To decline, say すみません, sorry, and then ちょっと, a little, and trail off: すみません、ちょっと……. Everyone understands this means “no”.

You can add a reason, such as “I have a test tomorrow”.

  • すみません、ちょっと……。
    Sumimasen, chotto...
    Sorry, that's a bit... (a polite no)
  • あしたは ちょっと……。
    Ashita wa chotto...
    Tomorrow is a bit difficult...

ませんか vs ますか

ますか asks a plain question: “Do you drink coffee?”. ませんか invites: “Would you like to drink coffee with me?”.

The meaning changes with only one small difference, so listen to the context.

  • コーヒーを のみますか。
    Koohii o nomimasu ka.
    Do you drink coffee? (question)
  • コーヒーを のみませんか。
    Koohii o nomimasen ka.
    Would you like to drink coffee? (invitation)

Common mistakes

Do not add です: のみませんですか is wrong. And do not use ませんか for a plain question about habits.

Keep it short, and add いっしょに if you want to be clear.

  • ✗ のみませんですか。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: no です.
  • いっしょに のみませんか。
    Issho ni nomimasen ka.
    Right: Shall we drink together?
Try it: Invite: “Shall we go together tomorrow?”

あした いっしょに いきませんか。

Ashita issho ni ikimasen ka.: Shall we go together tomorrow?

Vocabulary

  • いっしょにissho nitogether
  • ぜひzehiby all means
  • ちょっとchottoa little, a bit
  • どようびdoyoubiSaturday
  • にちようびnichiyoubiSunday
  • どうですかdou desu kahow about ...?
  • いつitsuwhen

Story: A movie invitation

  • Alex
    ミカさん、しゅうまつ えいがを みませんか。
    Mika-san, shuumatsu eiga o mimasen ka.
    Mika, would you like to watch a movie this weekend?
  • Mika
    いいですね。なにを みますか。
    Ii desu ne. Nani o mimasu ka.
    That sounds nice. What will we watch?
  • Alex
    にほんの えいがを みませんか。
    Nihon no eiga o mimasen ka.
    Shall we watch a Japanese movie?
  • Mika
    はい、ぜひ。いつ いきますか。
    Hai, zehi. Itsu ikimasu ka.
    Yes, by all means. When shall we go?
  • Alex
    どようびは どうですか。
    Doyoubi wa dou desu ka.
    How about Saturday?
  • Mika
    すみません、どようびは ちょっと……。にちようびは どうですか。
    Sumimasen, doyoubi wa chotto... Nichiyoubi wa dou desu ka.
    Sorry, Saturday is a bit difficult. How about Sunday?

Remember

  • ませんか invites: “Would you like to ...?” or “Shall we ...?”.
  • Accept with いいですね or ぜひ. Decline gently with すみません、ちょっと……
  • ますか asks a plain question; ませんか suggests.
