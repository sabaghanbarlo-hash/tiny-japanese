と: And, With

Goal: By the end you can list a complete set of nouns and say who you went with.

Watch the lesson

The lesson, step by step

Two meanings

と has two jobs. Between nouns it means “and”. After a person it means “with”. Both come from the same idea: putting two things together.

This lesson takes them one at a time.

  • パンと たまごを かいます。
    Pan to tamago o kaimasu.
    I buy bread and eggs.
  • ともだちと いきます。
    Tomodachi to ikimasu.
    I go with a friend.

Noun と noun

Between two nouns, と means “and”. It gives a complete list: exactly these things and nothing else. パンと たまご is bread and eggs, and no more.

Later you will learn や, which gives an incomplete list: “bread and eggs and so on”.

  • パンと たまごを かいます。
    Pan to tamago o kaimasu.
    I buy bread and eggs.
  • ミカさんと アレックスさんは がくせい です。
    Mika-san to Arekkusu-san wa gakusei desu.
    Mika and Alex are students.

Only nouns

と joins nouns only. You cannot use it to join two verbs or two adjectives. “I eat and drink” needs a different pattern, which you will learn with the te-form.

So たべますと のみます is wrong.

  • ✗ たべますと のみます。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: と does not join verbs.
  • パンと コーヒーを かいます。
    Pan to koohii o kaimasu.
    Right: I buy bread and coffee.

With a person

After a person, と means “with”. ともだちと いきます is “I go with a friend”. Your companion in the action takes と.

Notice the difference from に: に marks the person an action reaches, like the person you meet. と marks the person who joins you.

  • ともだちと いきます。
    Tomodachi to ikimasu.
    I go with a friend.
  • せんせいと はなします。
    Sensei to hanashimasu.
    I talk with the teacher.
  • かぞくと ごはんを たべます。
    Kazoku to gohan o tabemasu.
    I eat a meal with my family.

Alone and together

To say “alone”, use ひとりで, not と. To say “two people together”, use ふたりで. These use で, because the number of people works like the means of doing it.

So you can say who you did something with, or say you were alone.

  • ひとりで いきます。
    Hitori de ikimasu.
    I go alone.
  • ふたりで いきます。
    Futari de ikimasu.
    We (two) go together.

Who with: だれと

だれと means “with whom?”. だれと いきますか asks who you are going with.

The answer replaces だれ with a person, as in ケンさんと いきます.

  • だれと いきますか。
    Dare to ikimasu ka.
    Who are you going with?
  • ケンさんと いきます。
    Ken-san to ikimasu.
    I'm going with Ken.

Common mistakes

Do not use に to say “with a friend”. ともだちに いきます sounds like you go to the friend. Use と for company.

And do not put と after the last noun in a list: パンと たまごを, not パンと たまごと を.

  • ✗ ともだちに いきます。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: with a friend is と.
  • ✗ パンと たまごと を かいます。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: no と after the last noun.
  • ともだちと いきます。
    Tomodachi to ikimasu.
    Right: I go with a friend.
Try it: Say: “I buy bread and eggs.”

パンと たまごを かいます。

Pan to tamago o kaimasu.: I buy bread and eggs.

    Will you buy bread and coffee after the movie?
  • Alex
    はい、かいます。
    Hai, kaimasu.
    Yes, I will.

Remember

  • と joins nouns only. It does not join verbs.
  • だれと means “with whom?”, and the answer is ともだちと, かぞくと, and so on.
