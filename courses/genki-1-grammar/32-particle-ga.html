が: The Subject Marker

Goal: By the end you can answer who does something and say what exists.

Watch the lesson

The lesson, step by step

The idea: the doer, in focus

が marks the subject: who or what does the action, or what has the state. It shines a light on the doer. You already know は, which marks the topic. This lesson shows how the two feel different.

A simple way to start: が often answers the question “who?” or “what?”.

  • だれが コーヒーを いれますか。
    Dare ga koohii o iremasu ka.
    Who makes the coffee?
  • わたしが いれます。
    Watashi ga iremasu.
    I make it.

Question word + が

When the subject is a question word like だれ, who, or なに, what, use が, not は. だれが いれますか asks who makes it.

Never use は directly after だれ when the question asks who does something.

  • だれが いれますか。
    Dare ga iremasu ka.
    Who makes it?
  • だれが かいますか。
    Dare ga kaimasu ka.
    Who buys it?

Answering with が

The answer to a “who” question gets が too, because the new information is the doer. アレックスさんが いれます means Alex makes it, and nobody else.

The doer is the answer to the question, so it is in the spotlight.

  • アレックスさんが いれます。
    Arekkusu-san ga iremasu.
    Alex makes it.
  • わたしが かいます。
    Watashi ga kaimasu.
    I buy it.

Things that exist

が also marks the thing that exists with あります, there is. “There is a cake” is ケーキが あります. The thing that is there is the subject.

You will use this pattern constantly to say what is in a room, a bag or a town.

  • ケーキが あります。
    Keeki ga arimasu.
    There is a cake.
  • ほんが あります。
    Hon ga arimasu.
    There is a book.

は vs が

Here is a simple guide. は says “as for X”, a topic that is already known, and the new information is in the comment. が says “it is X”, and the new information is the subject itself.

わたしは いれます means “as for me, I make it”. わたしが いれます means “I am the one who makes it”.

  • わたしは いれます。
    Watashi wa iremasu.
    As for me, I make it.
  • わたしが いれます。
    Watashi ga iremasu.
    I'm the one who makes it.

Common mistakes

Do not ask a who-question with は: だれが is the normal form. And do not use を for something that exists. “There is a book” takes が.

If you are unsure whether to use は or が, ask: is the doer the answer to a question, or new information? Then use が.

  • ✗ だれは コーヒーを いれますか。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: a question word as the subject takes が.
  • ✗ ほんを あります。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: the thing that exists takes が.
  • ほんが あります。
    Hon ga arimasu.
    Right: There is a book.
Try it: Ask: “Who buys the cake?”

だれが ケーキを かいますか。

Dare ga keeki o kaimasu ka.: Who buys the cake?

Remember

  • Use が when the subject is a question word: だれが, なにが.
  • は is the topic and が is the doer. Use が to highlight who does it.
