Casual Speech: The Dictionary Form

Goal: By the end you can say casual sentences like たべる and いく and know when casual speech is appropriate.

Watch the lesson

The lesson, step by step

The idea

So far everything has been in the polite style, with です and ます. Japanese also has a plain, casual style used with family, close friends and in thoughts. Learning it is essential, because anime, songs and everyday conversation use it all the time.

The plain form of a verb is just the dictionary form that you already know.

  • たべます → たべる
    tabemasu → taberu
    eat (polite → casual)
  • いきます → いく
    ikimasu → iku
    go (polite → casual)

The dictionary form

The dictionary form is the plain present and future form. たべる means “eat” or “will eat”. のむ means “drink” or “will drink”. する means “do”. くる means “come”.

The meaning is the same as the ます form. Only the style changes.

  • あした いく。
    Ashita iku.
    (I) will go tomorrow.
  • まいにち のむ。
    Mainichi nomu.
    (I) drink every day.
  • みる？
    Miru?
    Will you watch it?

Questions

In casual speech you usually drop か and raise your voice at the end. たべる？ means “do you eat?”. In writing, a question mark shows that.

Friends might also add の: いくの？ sounds soft and friendly.

  • たべる？
    Taberu?
    Will you eat?
  • いっしょに いく？
    Issho ni iku?
    Will you go together?
  • なに たべる？
    Nani taberu?
    What will you eat?

Dropping particles

In casual speech, を, は and が are often left out. ごはん たべる？ sounds natural, with を removed. Particles of place, such as に, で, へ, usually stay.

Do not drop particles in writing or in polite speech.

  • ごはん たべる？
    Gohan taberu?
    Will you eat?
  • がっこうに いく。
    Gakkou ni iku.
    (I) go to school.
  • テレビ みる？
    Terebi miru?
    Will you watch TV?

When to use it

Use casual speech with family, close friends and people younger than you. Do not use it with a teacher, a boss, a shop assistant or someone you have just met. It can sound rude.

If in doubt, stay polite. It is better to be too polite than too casual.

  • せんせい、たべますか。
    Sensei, tabemasu ka.
    Teacher, will you eat? (polite)
  • ミカ、たべる？
    Mika, taberu?
    Mika, will you eat? (casual)

Common mistakes

Do not mix polite and casual in the same sentence: たべる です is wrong for a verb. And do not use casual speech to your teacher or customer.

Choose one style for the whole conversation.

  • ✗ たべる です。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: do not add です to a verb.
  • ✗ たべる ます。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: do not mix る and ます.
  • たべる。
    Taberu.
    Right: (I) eat.
Try it: Say casually: “Will you go?”

いく？

Iku?: Will you go?

Vocabulary

  • たべるtaberueat (plain)
  • いくikugo (plain)
  • のむnomudrink (plain)
  • みるmirusee, watch (plain)
  • するsurudo (plain)
  • うんunyeah (casual yes)
  • ううんuunnope (casual no)
  • ひまhimafree

Story: Between friends

  • Mika
    アレックス、あした ひま？
    Arekkusu, ashita hima?
    Alex, are you free tomorrow?
  • Alex
    うん、ひま。なに する？
    Un, hima. Nani suru?
    Yeah, I'm free. What shall we do?
  • Mika
    えいが みる？
    Eiga miru?
    Want to watch a movie?
  • Alex
    いいね。なに みる？
    Ii ne. Nani miru?
    Sounds good. What shall we watch?
  • Mika
    アニメ みる。
    Anime miru.
    Let's watch anime.
  • Alex
    うん、いく。
    Un, iku.
    Yeah, I'll go.

Remember

  • Plain present is the dictionary form: taberu, iku, nomu.
  • Questions use a rising tone, no ka.
  • Use casual speech only with close people.
