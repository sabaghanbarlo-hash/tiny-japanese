～たい: Wanting to Do

Goal: By the end you can say “I want to eat”, “I don't want to go” and ask “what do you want to do?”.

The lesson, step by step

The idea

To say that you want to do something, take the ます form, drop ます, and add たい. たべます becomes たべたい, “want to eat”. いきます becomes いきたい, “want to go”.

Add です to be polite: たべたい です.

  • たべます → たべたい
    tabemasu → tabetai
    eat → want to eat
  • いきます → いきたい
    ikimasu → ikitai
    go → want to go
  • ねます → ねたい
    nemasu → netai
    sleep → want to sleep

It is an い-adjective

たい behaves exactly like an い-adjective. So it changes the same way. Negative: たくない. Past: たかった. Negative past: たくなかった.

You already know all of these patterns from the adjective lessons.

  • たべたくない です。
    Tabetakunai desu.
    (I) don't want to eat.
  • たべたかった です。
    Tabetakatta desu.
    (I) wanted to eat.
  • たべたくなかった です。
    Tabetakunakatta desu.
    (I) didn't want to eat.

Objects

With たい, the object can be marked with を or が. Both are correct. すしを たべたい です and すしが たべたい です both mean “I want to eat sushi”.

The が version stresses the thing you want. Other particles, like に and で, do not change.

  • すしを たべたい です。
    Sushi o tabetai desu.
    I want to eat sushi.
  • すしが たべたい です。
    Sushi ga tabetai desu.
    I want to eat sushi.
  • にほんへ いきたい です。
    Nihon e ikitai desu.
    I want to go to Japan.

Questions

Ask なにが したい ですか for “what do you want to do?”. Ask なにを たべたい ですか for “what do you want to eat?”.

Using the たい form is perfectly polite when asking a friend, but be careful with seniors: it can sound direct.

  • なにが したい ですか。
    Nani ga shitai desu ka.
    What do you want to do?
  • なにを たべたい ですか。
    Nani o tabetai desu ka.
    What do you want to eat?
  • どこへ いきたい ですか。
    Doko e ikitai desu ka.
    Where do you want to go?

Only for yourself

たい describes your own feelings, or a question to the listener. You cannot say ミカさんは たべたい です as a plain statement about another person. For others you will learn たがっています later, or say たべたい と いって いました, “she said she wants to eat”.

This is because we cannot be sure about another person's inner feelings.

  • わたしは たべたい です。
    Watashi wa tabetai desu.
    I want to eat.
  • ✗ ミカさんは たべたい です。
    (wrong)
    Wrong as a statement about someone else.

Common mistakes

Do not use the dictionary form: たべるたい is wrong. And do not add です after たい in the negative as たいない; it is たくない.

Remember that たい is an い-adjective, so the negative is たくない.

  • ✗ たべるたい です。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: use the ます stem.
  • ✗ たべたい ない です。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: the negative is たくない.
  • たべたい です。
    Tabetai desu.
    Right: I want to eat.
Try it: Say: “I want to go to Japan.”

にほんへ いきたい です。

Nihon e ikitai desu.: I want to go to Japan.

Story: Dream trip

  • Mika
    アレックスさん、なつやすみに どこへ いきたい ですか。
    Arekkusu-san, natsuyasumi ni doko e ikitai desu ka.
    Alex, where do you want to go in summer vacation?
  • Alex
    にほんへ いきたい です。
    Nihon e ikitai desu.
    I want to go to Japan.
  • Mika
    なにを したい ですか。
    Nani o shitai desu ka.
    What do you want to do?
  • Alex
    おんせんに はいりたい です。そして ラーメンも たべたい です。
    Onsen ni hairitai desu. Soshite raamen mo tabetai desu.
    I want to go in a hot spring. And I want to eat ramen too.
  • Mika
    おみやげは かいたい ですか。
    Omiyage wa kaitai desu ka.
    Do you want to buy souvenirs?
  • Alex
    はい、たくさん かいたい です。
    Hai, takusan kaitai desu.
    Yes, I want to buy a lot.

Remember

  • Masu stem plus tai means want to.
  • Tai is an i-adjective: takunai, takatta, takunakatta.
  • Use it for your own wants and questions, not statements about others.
