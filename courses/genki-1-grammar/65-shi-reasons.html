～し: Listing Reasons

Goal: By the end you can say “it's cheap, and it's tasty, so I like it” using し.

The lesson, step by step

The idea

し joins reasons or qualities, like “and what's more”. やすい し、おいしい means “it's cheap, and it's delicious too”. It suggests there are even more reasons.

It attaches to the plain form, just like でしょう.

  • やすい し、おいしい です。
    Yasui shi, oishii desu.
    It's cheap, and it's delicious.
  • しずか だし、きれい です。
    Shizuka da shi, kirei desu.
    It's quiet, and it's pretty.

With verbs and adjectives

With verbs and い-adjectives, attach し to the plain form: いく し, たかい し, おいしかった し. With a negative, いかない し.

Add も to emphasise: えいがも みたし.

  • やすい し、おおきい し、いい です。
    Yasui shi, ookii shi, ii desu.
    It's cheap, it's big, and it's good.
  • さむい し、いかない。
    Samui shi, ikanai.
    It's cold, so I'm not going.
  • ひまだった し、いった。
    Hima datta shi, itta.
    I was free, so I went.

Nouns and な-adjectives

With nouns and な-adjectives, you keep the plain だ: しずか だし, がくせい だし. This is one of the few patterns where だ stays.

The past is だった し.

  • しずか だし、べんり です。
    Shizuka da shi, benri desu.
    It's quiet and it's convenient.
  • がくせい だし、おかねが ない。
    Gakusei da shi, okane ga nai.
    I'm a student, and I have no money.
  • きのうは あめ だった し、さむかった。
    Kinou wa ame datta shi, samukatta.
    Yesterday it rained, and it was cold.

Giving a reason

To give reasons for a decision, list them with し and finish with から: やすい し、おいしい から、ここが すき です means “it's cheap and it's delicious, so I like this place”.

から at the end of the list says “so”.

  • やすい し、おいしい から、ここが すき です。
    Yasui shi, oishii kara, koko ga suki desu.
    It's cheap and delicious, so I like this place.
  • じかんが ない し、いきません。
    Jikan ga nai shi, ikimasen.
    I have no time, so I'm not going.
  • さむい し、あめも ふって いる から、うちに います。
    Samui shi, ame mo futte iru kara, uchi ni imasu.
    It's cold and raining, so I'll stay home.

Giving an answer with one reason

Even one し can hint at more reasons. ちょっと たかい し... means “it's a bit expensive, and so on”, which can politely refuse something without saying so directly.

This is an indirect, polite way of saying no.

  • ちょっと たかい し...
    Chotto takai shi...
    It's a bit expensive, and so...
  • きょうは いそがしい し...
    Kyou wa isogashii shi...
    I'm busy today, and so...

Common mistakes

In this course, always use the plain form before し: いきます し is not the pattern we use. And do not drop だ with a noun: がくせい し is wrong.

A し list needs at least two reasons for the best effect.

  • ✗ いきます し、おいしい。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: use the plain form.
  • ✗ がくせい し、おかねが ない。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: keep だ.
  • やすい し、おいしい です。
    Yasui shi, oishii desu.
    Right: It's cheap and delicious.
Try it: Say: “It's cheap, and it's delicious.”

やすい し、おいしい です。

Yasui shi, oishii desu.: It's cheap, and it's delicious.

Story: Why I like this town

  • Alex
    どうして この まちが すきなんですか。
    Doushite kono machi ga suki na n desu ka.
    Why do you like this town?
  • Mika
    しずか だし、きれい だし、ひとも しんせつ ですから。
    Shizuka da shi, kirei da shi, hito mo shinsetsu desu kara.
    It's quiet, it's pretty, and the people are kind too.
  • Alex
    べんりですか。
    Benri desu ka.
    Is it convenient?
  • Mika
    えきも ちかい し、かいものも べんり です。
    Eki mo chikai shi, kaimono mo benri desu.
    The station is near, and shopping is convenient too.
  • Alex
    いいですね。わたしも すみたいです。
    Ii desu ne. Watashi mo sumitai desu.
    Nice. I'd like to live here too.
  • Mika
    ぜひ きて ください。
    Zehi kite kudasai.
    Please do come.

Remember

  • Plain form plus shi lists reasons: yasui shi, oishii.
  • Nouns and na-adjectives keep da: shizuka da shi.
  • Finish with kara to say so.
