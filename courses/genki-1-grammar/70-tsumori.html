～つもりです: I Plan To

Goal: By the end you can say “I plan to go to Kyoto” and “I don't plan to drink”.

The lesson, step by step

The idea

To say what you intend to do, put the plain present form of the verb before つもりです. いく つもりです means “I plan to go”.

It is about a decision you have already made, not just a wish.

  • らいしゅう にほんへ いく つもり です。
    Raishuu nihon e iku tsumori desu.
    I plan to go to Japan next week.
    Word by word

  • あした べんきょうする つもり です。
    Ashita benkyou suru tsumori desu.
    I plan to study tomorrow.
    Word by word

Which form?

Use the plain present form you learned earlier: たべる, のむ, する. Do not use the ます form.

So たべる つもり です, not たべます つもり です.

  • あさ ごはんを たべる つもり です。
    Asa gohan o taberu tsumori desu.
    I plan to eat breakfast.

Plans you won't do

To say you do not plan to do something, use the plain negative ない form. いかない つもり です means “I don't plan to go”.

The meaning is a firm decision not to do it.

  • パーティーへ いかない つもり です。
    Paatii e ikanai tsumori desu.
    I don't plan to go to the party.

Plans that changed

Use つもりでした for what you were planning. It often suggests the plan did not happen.

いく つもりでした means “I was planning to go”.

  • いく つもりでした。でも いきませんでした。
    Iku tsumori deshita. Demo ikimasen deshita.
    I was planning to go. But I didn't.
    Word by word
    いくikugoつもりtsumoriintentionでしたdeshitawas。でもdemobutいきませんでしたikimasen deshitadid not go。
  • べんきょうする つもりでした。
    Benkyou suru tsumori deshita.
    I was planning to study.
    Word by word
    べんきょうするbenkyō surustudyつもりtsumoriintentionでしたdeshitawas。

Common mistakes

Do not put the ます form before つもり. いきます つもり です is wrong. Use the plain form: いく つもり です.

Remember the topic of the plan comes first, then the plain verb.

  • ✗ いきます つもり です。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: use the plain form.
    Word by word
    いきますikimasugo politelyつもりtsumoriintentionですdesupolite copula。
  • いく つもり です。
    Iku tsumori desu.
    Right: I plan to go.
    Word by word
    いくikugoつもりtsumoriintentionですdesupolite copula。
Try it: Say: “I plan to eat sushi.”

Sushi o taberu tsumori desu.: I plan to eat sushi.

Remember

  • Plain present plus tsumori desu means I plan to.
  • For a firm no, use the plain negative: nai tsumori desu.
  • Never put the masu form before tsumori.
