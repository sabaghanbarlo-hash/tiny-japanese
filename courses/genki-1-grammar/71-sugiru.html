～すぎます: Too Much

Goal: By the end you can say “I ate too much” and “This is too expensive”.

The lesson, step by step

The idea

Add すぎます to a verb or an adjective to say “too much” or “too”. たべすぎます means “eat too much”. たかすぎます means “too expensive”.

It always means more than is good.

  • ごはんを たべすぎました。
    Gohan o tabesugimashita.
    I ate too much.

With verbs

Take the ます form, remove ます, and add すぎます. たべます becomes たべすぎます. のみます becomes のみすぎます. します becomes しすぎます.

すぎる is an いちだん verb, so it ends with すぎます, すぎました and すぎない.

  • ジュースを のみすぎました。
    Juusu o nomisugimashita.
    I drank too much juice.
    Word by word

  • きのう あそびすぎました。
    Kinou asobisugimashita.
    I played too much yesterday.
    Word by word
    きのうkinōyesterdayあそびすぎましたasobisugimashitaplayed too much。

With い-adjectives

For an い-adjective, drop the last い and add すぎます. たかい becomes たかすぎます. あつい becomes あつすぎます. ちいさい becomes ちいさすぎます.

The word いい changes to よ, so “too good” is よすぎます.

  • きょうは あつすぎます。
    Kyou wa atsusugimasu.
    Today is too hot.

With な-adjectives

For a な-adjective, simply add すぎます to the stem, with no な. しずか becomes しずかすぎます. にぎやか becomes にぎやかすぎます.

No other change is needed.

    It is too quiet here.

Asking not to

You can join it to the lesson on asking people not to do things. たべすぎないで ください means “please don't eat too much”.

It is a polite request about the amount.

  • たべすぎないで ください。
    Tabesuginai de kudasai.
    Please don't eat too much.

Common mistakes

Do not keep the ます ending: たべますすぎます is wrong. Do not keep the final い of an い-adjective: たかいすぎます is wrong.

Cut the ending first, then add すぎます.

  • ✗ たべますすぎました。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: remove ます first.
    Word by word
    たべますtabemasueatすぎましたsugimashitaoverdid it。
  • ✗ たかいすぎます。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: drop the い.
    Word by word
    たかいtakaihigh; expensiveすぎますsugimasuis too much。
  • たべすぎました。
    Tabesugimashita.
    Right: I ate too much.
    Word by word
    たべすぎましたtabesugimashitaate too much。

Remember

  • Verbs: remove masu and add sugimasu. I-adjectives: drop the i.
  • Na-adjectives keep their stem: shizuka sugimasu.
  • Ii becomes yo: yosugimasu means too good.
