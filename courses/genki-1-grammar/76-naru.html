～に / く なります: Become

Goal: By the end you can say “It became cold” and “I want to become a teacher”.

The lesson, step by step

The idea

なります means “become” or “get”. The word before it changes depending on its type.

さむく なります means “it gets cold”. げんきに なります means “I become well”.

  • さむく なりました。
    Samuku narimashita.
    It became cold.
  • げんきに なりました。
    Genki ni narimashita.
    I became well.

い-adjectives

For an い-adjective, drop the last い and add く, then なります. あつい becomes あつく なります. ながい becomes ながく なります.

This is the same く you used in the negative.

  • あつく なります。
    Atsuku narimasu.
    It gets hot.
  • よるは さむく なります。
    Yoru wa samuku narimasu.
    It gets cold at night.

な-adjectives and nouns

For a な-adjective or a noun, add に. げんき becomes げんきに なります. いしゃ, “doctor”, becomes いしゃに なります.

So に marks what you become.

  • まちが しずかに なりました。
    Machi ga shizuka ni narimashita.
    The town became quiet.
  • いしゃに なります。
    Isha ni narimasu.
    I will become a doctor.

いい is special

The adjective いい changes to よ in this pattern. よく なります means “gets better”.

Use it for health, weather or skill.

  • よく なりました。
    Yoku narimashita.
    It got better.
  • ねつが よく なりました。
    Netsu ga yoku narimashita.
    The fever got better.

Wanting to become

Join it with たい: なりたい means “want to become”. いしゃに なりたい です means “I want to become a doctor”.

It is a nice way to talk about dreams.

  • いしゃに なりたい です。
    Isha ni naritai desu.
    I want to become a doctor.
  • にほんごが じょうずに なりたい です。
    Nihongo ga jouzu ni naritai desu.
    I want to get good at Japanese.

Common mistakes

Do not use に after an い-adjective: さむいに なります is wrong; use さむく. Do not use く after a な-adjective or a noun: げんきく なります is wrong; use げんきに.

Check the type of the word first.

  • ✗ さむいに なりました。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: use く.
  • ✗ げんきく なりました。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: use に.
  • さむく なりました。
    Samuku narimashita.
    Right: It became cold.
Try it: Say: “It became warm.”

あたたかく なりました。

Atatakaku narimashita.: It became warm.

Remember

  • I-adjectives: drop i, add ku, then narimasu.
  • Na-adjectives and nouns: add ni, then narimasu.
  • Ii becomes yoku: yoku narimashita means it got better.
