ごろ と ぐらい: About

Goal: By the end you can say “about three o'clock” and “about two hours”.

The lesson, step by step

The idea

ごろ means “about” or “around” a point in time. Put it after a clock time. さんじごろ means “around three o'clock”.

It also works with days, like にちようびごろ, but you will mostly use it with clock times.

  • さんじごろ かえります。
    Sanji goro kaerimasu.
    I'll go home around three.
  • しちじごろ おきます。
    Shichiji goro okimasu.
    I get up around seven.

Amounts

ぐらい means “about” for an amount or a length of time. にじかんぐらい means “about two hours”. ひゃくえんぐらい means “about a hundred yen”.

It is also written くらい. The two spellings mean the same.

  • にじかんぐらい べんきょうします。
    Nijikan gurai benkyou shimasu.
    I study for about two hours.
  • ひゃくえんぐらい です。
    Hyaku en gurai desu.
    It's about a hundred yen.

Which one?

Ask yourself: is it a point on the clock, or an amount? A point uses ごろ: さんじごろ. An amount uses ぐらい: さんじかんぐらい.

さんじ is three o'clock. さんじかん is three hours.

  • さんじごろ きます。
    Sanji goro kimasu.
    I'll come around three o'clock.
  • さんじかんぐらい まちました。
    Sanjikan gurai machimashita.
    I waited about three hours.

How long?

どのぐらい asks “about how much?” or “about how long?”. どのぐらい かかりますか means “About how long does it take?”.

A natural answer is an amount with ぐらい.

  • どのぐらい かかりますか。
    Dono gurai kakarimasu ka.
    About how long does it take?
  • さんじゅっぷんぐらい かかります。
    Sanjuppun gurai kakarimasu.
    It takes about thirty minutes.

Common mistakes

Do not use ごろ for an amount of time: にじかんごろ is wrong; say にじかんぐらい. Do not use ぐらい to say a clock time precisely in careful speech; さんじごろ is the standard way.

Points use ごろ. Amounts use ぐらい.

  • ✗ にじかんごろ べんきょうします。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: use ぐらい for an amount.
  • にじかんぐらい べんきょうします。
    Nijikan gurai benkyou shimasu.
    Right: I study about two hours.
Try it: Say: “I get up at about seven.”

しちじごろ おきます。

Shichiji goro okimasu.: I get up at about seven.

Vocabulary

  • じかんjikanhours, time

Remember

  • Goro follows a point in time, such as sanji goro.
  • Gurai follows an amount, such as nijikan gurai.
  • Dono gurai asks about how much or how long.
