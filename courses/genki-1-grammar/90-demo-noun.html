～でも: Tea or Something

Goal: By the end you can say “Shall we have tea or something?” and “Even a child can do it”.

The lesson, step by step

The idea

Put でも after a noun to make a soft suggestion, like “or something”. おちゃでも のみませんか means “Shall we have tea or something?”.

It does not say it must be tea. It leaves the choice open and friendly.

  • おちゃでも のみませんか。
    Ocha demo nomimasen ka.
    Shall we have tea or something?
  • コーヒーでも のみましょう。
    Koohii demo nomimashou.
    Let's have coffee or something.

Inviting someone

It is a nice way to invite. えいがでも みませんか means “How about a movie or something?”.

It is gentle, because it does not push one exact plan.

  • えいがでも みませんか。
    Eiga demo mimasen ka.
    How about a movie or something?
  • さんぽでも しませんか。
    Sanpo demo shimasen ka.
    How about a walk or something?

Particles

でも replaces を and が. Do not say おちゃをでも. Just say おちゃでも.

Other particles, like に and へ, can stay before it.

  • おちゃでも のみませんか。
    Ocha demo nomimasen ka.
    Shall we have tea or something?
  • ケーキでも たべましょう。
    Keeki demo tabemashou.
    Let's eat cake or something.

A second meaning: even

でも after a noun can also mean “even”. こどもでも できます means “Even a child can do it”.

The suggestion meaning usually comes with a verb like のみませんか. The “even” meaning shows a surprising case.

  • こどもでも できます。
    Kodomo demo dekimasu.
    Even a child can do it.
  • にほんごでも できます。
    Nihongo demo dekimasu.
    I can do it even in Japanese.

Not the same as でも (but)

You already know でも at the start of a sentence, meaning “but”. That is a different use. でも、たかい です means “But it's expensive”.

Look at where it stands. At the start it means “but”. Right after a noun it means “or something” or “even”.

  • でも、たかい です。
    Demo, takai desu.
    But it's expensive.
  • おちゃでも どうですか。
    Ocha demo dou desu ka.
    How about tea or something?

Common mistakes

Do not add を: おちゃを でも のみませんか is wrong.

Noun first, then でも.

  • ✗ おちゃを でも のみませんか。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: drop を.
  • おちゃでも のみませんか。
    Ocha demo nomimasen ka.
    Right.
Try it: Say: “Shall we have tea or something?”

おちゃでも のみませんか。

Ocha demo nomimasen ka.: Shall we have tea or something?

Remember

  • Noun plus demo makes a soft suggestion: ocha demo.
  • Demo replaces o and ga.
  • At the start of a sentence, demo means but.
