に: Time, Goal and Target

Goal: By the end you can say when you do something and where you go.

Watch the lesson

Turn your sound on: listen to each sound, then say it out loud yourself. Tap any character or speaker button on this page to hear it again. Voices: Mika and Alex (neural text-to-speech).

The lesson, step by step

The big idea: a point

Learners are often told that に has ten different meanings. Here we will start with one picture instead: に points at a single dot. It can be a dot in time, a dot in space, or a person the action reaches.

Imagine an arrow landing on that dot. A time, a place you arrive at, a person you meet. That one picture will carry you through most of what you meet in Genki one.

  • しちじに おきます。
    Shichiji ni okimasu.
    I get up at seven. (a point in time)
  • がっこうに いきます。
    Gakkou ni ikimasu.
    I go to school. (a point in space)
  • ともだちに あいます。
    Tomodachi ni aimasu.
    I meet a friend. (a person)

Use 1: specific times

After an exact time, put に. This works for clock times, days of the week and dates. Say the time, then に, then the verb.

しちじ is seven o'clock, and げつようび is Monday. The sentence always ends with the verb.

  • しちじに おきます。
    Shichiji ni okimasu.
    I get up at seven.
  • げつようびに ともだちに あいます。
    Getsuyoubi ni tomodachi ni aimasu.
    I meet a friend on Monday.
  • ごごに いきます。
    Gogo ni ikimasu.
    I go in the afternoon.

Times that do not take に

Words like today, tomorrow, yesterday and every day do not take に. Why? They are not exact points on the calendar or the clock. They are labels that move with the speaker. So you say あした いきます, not あしたに いきます.

A good rule: exact time, add に. Words like tomorrow, today and every day, leave it out.

  • あした いきます。
    Ashita ikimasu.
    I'll go tomorrow.
  • きょう おきます。
    Kyou okimasu.
    I get up today.
  • ✗ あしたに いきます。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: あした does not take に.

Use 2: destination

With verbs of movement, に marks the place you arrive at. School に go. Home に return. The place is where the movement ends.

Later you will learn へ, which can replace に here. For now, に is the safe choice.

  • がっこうに いきます。
    Gakkou ni ikimasu.
    I go to school.
  • うちに かえります。
    Uchi ni kaerimasu.
    I go home.
  • にほんに きます。
    Nihon ni kimasu.
    I come to Japan.

Use 3: the person you meet

に also marks the person an action reaches. The most common example is あいます, to meet. In English you just say “meet a friend”, with nothing in between. In Japanese you must add に.

Later you will use the same に for giving things to someone and for asking someone.

  • ともだちに あいます。
    Tomodachi ni aimasu.
    I meet a friend.
  • せんせいに あいます。
    Sensei ni aimasu.
    I meet the teacher.

に or で?

This is the biggest mix-up. に is for a destination: the point you go to. で is for the place where an action takes place. Going to the library is に. Studying in the library is で.

Ask yourself: am I going there, or doing something there? Going there is に. Doing something there is で. The next lesson is all about で.

  • としょかんに いきます。
    Toshokan ni ikimasu.
    I go to the library.
  • としょかんで べんきょうします。
    Toshokan de benkyou shimasu.
    I study at the library.
  • ✗ がっこうに べんきょうします。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: studying is an action, so use で.

Word order

When a sentence has a time and a place, the usual order is time first, then place, then the verb at the end. The time is like a frame around the whole sentence.

Each one gets its own に, and the verb always comes last.

  • しちじはんに がっこうに いきます。
    Shichiji han ni gakkou ni ikimasu.
    I go to school at seven thirty.
  • ごごに ともだちに あいます。
    Gogo ni tomodachi ni aimasu.
    I meet a friend in the afternoon.
Try it: Say: “I meet a friend at three p.m.”

ごご さんじに ともだちに あいます。

Gogo sanji ni tomodachi ni aimasu.: I meet a friend at three p.m.

Common mistakes

First, do not use を for the person you meet. English says “meet a friend”, but Japanese says “friend に meet”.

Second, do not put に after tomorrow, today or yesterday. Third, do not use に for the place where you study or eat. That is で.

  • ✗ ともだちを あいます。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: あいます needs に.
  • ともだちに あいます。
    Tomodachi ni aimasu.
    Right: I meet a friend.
  • ✗ あしたに いきます。
    (wrong)
    Wrong: あした does not take に.
Try it: Say: “I'm going home tomorrow.”

あした うちに かえります。

Ashita uchi ni kaerimasu.: I'm going home tomorrow.

    Mika-san wa nanji ni okimasu ka.

    Shichiji ni okimasu.
    I wake up at seven.

  • Mika

    Shichiji han ni gakkou ni ikimasu.
    I go to school at seven thirty.
  • Alex
    ごごは なにを しますか。
    Gogo wa nani o shimasu ka.
    What do you do in the afternoon?

    ごごに ともだちに あいます。
    Gogo ni tomodachi ni aimasu.
    In the afternoon I meet a friend.

Remember

  • Use に with clock times and days of the week, but not with きょう or あした.
  • あいます needs に for the person you meet: ともだちに あいます.
