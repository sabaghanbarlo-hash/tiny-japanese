は, です, か and の: Your First Sentences

Goal: By the end you can introduce yourself, ask a yes/no question and say whose something is.

Watch the lesson

Warm-up

Every Japanese sentence in Genki I starts from four small words: は, です, か and の. With just these you can introduce yourself, ask a question and say who something belongs to.

Listen to Mika and Alex meet for the first time, and see how many of the four you can spot.

Dialogue: Nice to meet you

  • Mika
    はじめまして。ミカです。よろしく おねがいします。
    Hajimemashite. Mika desu. Yoroshiku onegaishimasu.
    Nice to meet you. I'm Mika. Please treat me kindly.
  • Alex
    はじめまして。アレックスです。アメリカじん です。
    Hajimemashite. Arekkusu desu. Amerikajin desu.
    Nice to meet you. I'm Alex. I'm American.
  • Mika
    アレックスさんは だいがくせい ですか。
    Arekkusu-san wa daigakusei desu ka.
    Alex, are you a university student?
  • Alex
    はい、そうです。だいがくの いちねんせい です。
    Hai, sou desu. Daigaku no ichinensei desu.
    Yes, that's right. I'm a first-year university student.
  • Mika
    わたしは こうこうせい です。
    Watashi wa koukousei desu.
    I'm a high school student.
  • Alex
    ミカさんの せんせいは にほんじん ですか。
    Mika-san no sensei wa nihonjin desu ka.
    Is your teacher Japanese, Mika?
  • Mika
    はい、にほんじん です。アレックスさんの せんせいは？
    Hai, nihonjin desu. Arekkusu-san no sensei wa?
    Yes, she's Japanese. And your teacher, Alex?
  • Alex
    わたしの にほんごの せんせいは にほんじん です。
    Watashi no nihongo no sensei wa nihonjin desu.
    My Japanese teacher is Japanese.

Grammar and usage

は: the topic marker

は marks the topic, the thing you are talking about. It is written は but read “wa”. ミカさんは こうこうせい です means “As for Mika, she is a high school student”. Japanese does not need a subject in every sentence: if the topic is clear, you can leave it out.

です: the polite “is / am / are”

です goes at the very end and makes the sentence polite. It does not change for I, you, he or they: わたしは がくせい です。 ミカさんは がくせい です。 On its own, そうです means “That's right”.

か: the question marker

Put か at the end to ask a question: だいがくせい ですか。 You do not need a question mark, and your voice rises a little. Answer with はい (yes) or いいえ (no).

の: linking two nouns

の joins two nouns, and the first one describes or owns the second: わたしの せんせい is “my teacher” and にほんごの せんせい is “a Japanese teacher”. You can chain them: わたしの にほんごの せんせい (my Japanese teacher). The order is the reverse of English “teacher of Japanese”.

Culture note

  • End a self-introduction with よろしく おねがいします. It means something like “I hope we get along well”, and it is the normal polite way to finish.
  • Add さん to other people's names, never to your own. You say ミカです, not ミカさんです, about yourself.
