Pointing Words, も, じゃ ありません, ね and よ

Goal: By the end you can point at things, ask whose they are, say no politely, and sound natural with ね and よ.

Warm-up

Japanese has a tidy set of pointing words, called こそあど words after their first sounds. こ is close to me, そ is close to you, あ is far from both of us, and ど means “which”. Learn the pattern once and you get a whole family of words.

Alex and Mika are in a gift shop. Listen for the pointing words, for も, and for the little endings ね and よ.

Dialogue: At the gift shop

  • Alex
    すみません、これは なん ですか。
    Sumimasen, kore wa nan desu ka.
    Excuse me, what is this?
  • Mika
    それは せんす です。
    Sore wa sensu desu.
    That is a folding fan.
  • Alex
    あの かばんは だれの ですか。
    Ano kaban wa dare no desu ka.
    Whose is that bag over there?
  • Mika
    わたしの じゃ ありません。ともだちの です。
    Watashi no ja arimasen. Tomodachi no desu.
    It's not mine. It's my friend's.
  • Alex
    その かさも ともだちの ですか。
    Sono kasa mo tomodachi no desu ka.
    Is that umbrella your friend's too?
  • Mika
    いいえ、この かさは わたしの ですよ。
    Iie, kono kasa wa watashi no desu yo.
    No, this umbrella is mine, you know.
  • Alex
    そう ですか。レジは あそこ ですね。
    Sou desu ka. Reji wa asoko desu ne.
    I see. The register is over there, right?
  • Mika
    ええ、あそこ です。
    Ee, asoko desu.
    Yes, it's over there.

Grammar and usage

これ, それ, あれ, どれ: pointing at things

These stand alone and mean “this one, that one, that one over there, which one”. Use これ for things near you, それ for things near the person you are talking to, and あれ for things far from both. When you hold a pen and a friend asks about it, your friend says それ and you say これ.

この, その, あの, どの and ここ, そこ, あそこ, どこ

この, その, あの and どの describe a noun, so a noun must follow: この かばん (this bag). これ stands alone, but この never does. For places, use ここ, そこ, あそこ and どこ: レジは あそこ です。

だれの: whose

だれの means “whose”: あの かばんは だれの ですか。 Answer with a name plus の: ともだちの です。 If the thing is already clear, you can leave the noun out, so わたしの です means “It's mine”.

も: also

Replace は with も to say “too”: その かさも ともだちの ですか。 means “Is that umbrella also your friend's?” Use も only when the same thing is true of both items.

じゃ ありません: “is not”

To say “is not”, change です to じゃ ありません: わたしの じゃ ありません。 Careful writing uses では ありません. With close friends you will hear the short form じゃ ない, which you meet properly in Lesson 8.

ね: “isn't it? / right?”

ね at the end asks for agreement or shows you share a feeling: レジは あそこ ですね。 means “The register is over there, right?” Use it when you expect the other person to agree.

よ: “you know / I'm telling you”

よ at the end tells the listener something they may not know, or adds emphasis: この かさは わたしの ですよ。 Use it with care: too strong a よ can sound pushy.

Culture note

  • In many shops staff say いらっしゃいませ when you walk in. You do not have to answer; a small nod is enough.
  • ね is about sharing: you will hear it all day in friendly chat. よ is about informing. Choose between them by asking yourself “do I expect agreement, or am I telling them something new?”
