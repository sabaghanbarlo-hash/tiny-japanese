Verbs and Particles: を, で, に and へ

Goal: By the end you can say what you do, where and when, and invite a friend to do something.

Warm-up

Now we move from nouns to verbs. This is a big lesson, so go slowly. In Japanese the verb comes last, and small words called particles tell you the job of each word before it: the object, the place, the time, the direction.

Alex wants to invite Mika to the cinema. Listen for how he asks, how she answers, and how they agree on a time.

Dialogue: A movie invitation

  • Alex
    ミカさん、あした なにを しますか。
    Mika-san, ashita nani o shimasu ka.
    Mika, what are you doing tomorrow?
  • Mika
    あしたの ごぜん、うちで ほんを よみます。
    Ashita no gozen, uchi de hon o yomimasu.
    Tomorrow morning I'll read a book at home.
  • Alex
    そう ですか。ごご、えいがを みませんか。
    Sou desu ka. Gogo, eiga o mimasen ka.
    I see. Would you like to watch a movie in the afternoon?
  • Mika
    いいですね。コーヒーも のみませんか。
    Ii desu ne. Koohii mo nomimasen ka.
    Sounds good. Shall we have coffee too?
  • Alex
    ええ、のみます。
    Ee, nomimasu.
    Yes, let's.
  • Mika
    なんじに いきますか。
    Nanji ni ikimasu ka.
    What time shall we go?
  • Alex
    さんじに えきで あいませんか。
    Sanji ni eki de aimasen ka.
    Shall we meet at the station at three?
  • Mika
    はい、いいですよ。
    Hai, ii desu yo.
    Yes, that's fine.

Grammar and usage

Ichidan verbs (る-verbs)

Dictionary-form verbs end in the sound u. An ichidan verb ends in る and the sound before it is い or え: たべる, みる, ねる, おきる. They are the easy group because only the る changes.

Godan verbs (う-verbs)

Every other verb is godan: のむ, よむ, いく, はなす, あう. A few look ichidan but are godan, and the one to remember is かえる (go home). When you meet a verb ending in る, check it against your word list.

Irregular verbs

Only two verbs are irregular: する (do) and くる (come). Learn them as separate words.

Polite form: ichidan verbs

Drop る and add ます: たべる → たべます, みる → みます. The negative is ません: たべません.

Polite form: godan verbs

Change the last sound to the い-row, then add ます: のむ → のみます, よむ → よみます, いく → いきます, はなす → はなします, あう → あいます, かえる → かえります. Negative: のみません.

Polite form: irregular verbs

する → します and くる → きます (read “kimasu”). Negative: しません, きません.

Present tense: ます and ません

The ます form covers the present and the future, so あした えいがを みます means “I'll watch a movie tomorrow” and まいにち のみます means “I drink every day”. The negative is ません. The verb does not change for I, you or they.

を: the object

を marks what the action is done to: ほんを よみます。 It is written を but read “o”.

で: place or tool

で marks the place where an action happens: うちで ほんを よみます。 It also marks the tool or means: バスで いきます (go by bus).

に: time and target

に marks a specific time: さんじに あいます。 It also marks the destination of movement (がっこうに いきます) and the person you meet (ともだちに あいます). Words like あした and まいにち take no に.

へ: direction

へ marks the direction of movement: えきへ いきます。 It is written へ but read “e”. For going places, へ and に are often interchangeable.

～ませんか: an invitation

Add か to the negative form to invite politely: えいがを みませんか。 means “Would you like to watch a movie?” A friendly yes is ええ、みます or はい、いいですよ。

Frequency words

いつも, よく and ときどき go before the verb: ときどき えいがを みます。 With あまり and ぜんぜん the verb must be negative: ぜんぜん コーヒーを のみません。

Word order

A reliable order is topic, time, place, object, verb: ミカさんは あした うちで ほんを よみます。 Only the verb must stay last; the other parts can move if the particles stay attached.

Culture note

  • ませんか is a soft invitation, so a refusal is often soft too. People say ちょっと… (“it's a bit…”) and leave it unfinished instead of saying no. Listen for it.
  • かえる (go home) is a godan verb even though it ends in える. It is the most common one to get wrong.
