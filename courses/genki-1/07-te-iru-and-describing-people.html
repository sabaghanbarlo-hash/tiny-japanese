Lesson 7 of 12 · 40 min

ている, Describing People and Counting People

Goal: By the end you can say what someone is doing, describe a person, and talk about your family.

Warm-up

Now the て form gets a partner: いる. Together, ～ている describes an action in progress, but also a state, like where someone lives. You will also learn to describe people and count them.

Alex is looking at a family photo. Listen for ～ています, the descriptions, and the numbers of people.

Dialogue: A family photo

  • Alex
    ミカさん、これは ミカさんの かぞくの しゃしん ですか。
    Mika-san, kore wa Mika-san no kazoku no shashin desu ka.
    Mika, is this a photo of your family?
  • Mika
    ええ。いもうとは いま アメリカに すんで います。
    Ee. Imouto wa ima Amerika ni sunde imasu.
    Yes. My younger sister lives in America now.
  • Alex
    そう ですか。かぞくは なんにん ですか。
    Sou desu ka. Kazoku wa nannin desu ka.
    I see. How many people are in your family?
  • Mika
    よにん です。ちちと ははと いもうとと わたし です。
    Yonin desu. Chichi to haha to imouto to watashi desu.
    Four. My father, my mother, my sister and me.
  • Alex
    いもうとさんは かみが ながいですね。
    Imouto-san wa kami ga nagai desu ne.
    Your sister has long hair, doesn't she?
  • Mika
    ええ、かみが ながくて、せが たかいです。
    Ee, kami ga nagakute, se ga takai desu.
    Yes, she has long hair and she's tall.
  • Alex
    ミカさんの うちへ あそびに いっても いいですか。
    Mika-san no uchi e asobi ni itte mo ii desu ka.
    May I come over to your house to hang out?
  • Mika
    ええ、あそびに きて ください。
    Ee, asobi ni kite kudasai.
    Sure, please come over.

Grammar and usage

～ている: in progress and in a state

て form plus います shows an action in progress: いま たべて います (I'm eating now). With some verbs it shows a state that continues: すんで います (live), けっこんして います (is married), もって います (has). One to memorize: “to know” is しって います, but “don't know” is しりません.

～ている with いく and くる

With いく and くる, ～ている shows the resulting state, not an action in progress. ミカさんは もう きて います means “Mika has already come (and is here)”. アレックスさんは がっこうへ いって います means “Alex has gone to school (and is there)”.

～ていない: not doing, not yet done

The negative is ていません: まだ たべて いません means “I haven't eaten yet”. It is the usual way to say something has not happened yet.

は～が adjective: describing a feature

Say who you are describing with は, then the feature with が: いもうとさんは かみが ながいです。 (As for your sister, her hair is long.) ミカさんは せが たかいです。

Adjective and noun て forms

To join descriptions, an い-adjective changes い to くて: ながい → ながくて. いい is よくて. A な-adjective or a noun takes で: しずかで きれい です。 がくせいで アメリカじん です。

～に いく: go to do

Take the ます stem (the verb without ます) and add に いきます: えいがを みに いきます (go to watch a movie), あそびに いきます (go to hang out). With nouns, add に: かいものに いきます (go shopping).

Counting people

One person is ひとり and two is ふたり. After that, add にん: さんにん, よにん, ごにん… Ask なんにん ですか。 Watch out: four is よにん, not よんにん.

Culture note

  • Japanese has separate words for your own family and for someone else's. You say ちち and はは about your own parents, but おとうさん and おかあさん about someone else's. This is why Alex says いもうとさん when he talks about Mika's sister.
  • Visiting a Japanese home, you take off your shoes at the entrance (げんかん). Wait for the host to point you to the slippers.
