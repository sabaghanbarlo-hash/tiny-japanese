This, That and Where

Goal: By the end you can ask “What is this?” and “Where is it?” and understand the answers.

Watch the lesson

Watch the story, learn the words and grammar, then try the quiz.

Warm-up

Japanese has a tidy set of pointing words that start with こ, そ, あ and ど. こ means near me, そ means near you, あ means far from both of us, and ど means “which” or “where”. Learn the pattern once and you get a whole family of words.

Dialogue: At school

  • Alex
    すみません、これは なん ですか。
    Sumimasen, kore wa nan desu ka.
    Excuse me, what is this?
  • Mika
    それは けしゴム です。
    Sore wa keshigomu desu.
    That is an eraser.
  • Alex
    あれは なん ですか。
    Are wa nan desu ka.
    What is that over there?
  • Mika
    あれは とけい です。
    Are wa tokei desu.
    That is a clock.
  • Alex
    トイレは どこ ですか。
    Toire wa doko desu ka.
    Where is the restroom?
  • Mika
    あそこ です。あの ドアの よこ です。
    Asoko desu. Ano doa no yoko desu.
    It's over there. It's next to that door.
  • Alex
    ありがとう ございます。
    Arigatou gozaimasu.
    Thank you very much.

Vocabulary

  • これ / それ / あれkore / sore / arethis / that / that over there
  • どれdorewhich one
  • この / その / あのkono / sono / anothis / that / that over there (+ noun)
  • ここ / そこ / あそこkoko / soko / asokohere / there / over there
  • どこdokowhere
  • なんnanwhat
  • けしゴムkeshigomueraser
  • とけいtokeiclock, watch
  • トイレtoirerestroom
  • よこyokonext to, beside

Grammar and usage

The こそあど system

こ = close to the speaker, そ = close to the listener, あ = far from both, ど = question. これ, それ, あれ, どれ are things. ここ, そこ, あそこ, どこ are places.

これ stands alone, この needs a noun

Use これ by itself: これは ほん です。 Use この with a noun right after it: この ほんは わたしの です。 The same goes for その and あの.

What and where

Ask about things with なん ですか and about places with どこ ですか。 For “where is X?” say X は どこ ですか。 The answer can be ここ, そこ or あそこ です.

Place words with の

To say “next to the door” put の between the two nouns: ドアの よこ. You will meet の properly in the next lesson.

Tip

  • Politeness helper: すみません at the start gets someone's attention, just like “excuse me” in English.
