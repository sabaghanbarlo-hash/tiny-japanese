の, も and と

Goal: By the end you can say “my bag”, “Mika's book”, “this too” and “a pen and a notebook”.

Watch the lesson

Watch the story, learn the words and grammar, then try the quiz.

Warm-up

Three tiny particles do a lot of work. の links two nouns (often “of” or “'s”), も means “also”, and と means “and”. Mika and Alex sort out whose things are on the table.

Dialogue: Whose is it?

  • Alex
    これは わたしの かばん です。
    Kore wa watashi no kaban desu.
    This is my bag.
  • Mika
    それも アレックスさんの ですか。
    Sore mo Arekkusu-san no desu ka.
    Is that yours too?
  • Alex
    いいえ、それは ミカさんの です。
    Iie, sore wa Mika-san no desu.
    No, that is Mika's.
  • Mika
    あ、そう です。わたしの ほん と ノート です。
    A, sou desu. Watashi no hon to nooto desu.
    Oh, that's right. It's my book and notebook.
  • Alex
    これも ミカさんの ですか。
    Kore mo Mika-san no desu ka.
    Is this Mika's too?
  • Mika
    いいえ、それは アレックスさんの です。
    Iie, sore wa Arekkusu-san no desu.
    No, that's Alex's.

Grammar and usage

の links two nouns

A の B means “A's B” or “the B of A”: わたしの かばん (my bag), ミカさんの ほん (Mika's book), にほんごの ほん (a Japanese book). The order is the opposite of “book of Mika”: owner first.

の can replace the noun

When the noun is obvious you can drop it and keep の: それは アレックスさんの です。 means “That is Alex's.”

も replaces は

も means “also, too”. Use it instead of は, not after it: わたしも がくせい です。 means “I am a student too.”

と joins nouns

と means “and” between nouns only: ほんと ノート, ペンと さいふ. It does not join sentences or verbs.

Tip

  • In the dialogue, ほん と ノート has spaces to show you the words. In normal writing there is no space: ほんと ノート.
