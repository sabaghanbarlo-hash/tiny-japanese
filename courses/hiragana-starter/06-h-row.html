The H Row: は ひ ふ へ ほ

Goal: Read は ひ ふ へ ほ and know when は and へ change their sound.

The H row

Two special sounds

ふ is “fu”

Not a sharp English “f”. Blow softly as if you were cooling soup, with your lips nearly together. That is the Japanese “fu”.

は and へ as particles

When は marks the topic of a sentence it is read “wa”, not “ha”. When へ marks a direction (“to”) it is read “e”, not “he”. Everywhere else they keep their normal reading. You'll see this in almost every sentence you read.

Words you can read now

  • はなhanaflower
  • ひとhitoperson
  • ふねfuneship, boat
  • ほしhoshistar
  • はしhashichopsticks
  • ふくfukuclothes
  • ひくいhikuilow
  • へたhetaunskilled, poor at
  • ほしいhoshiiwant

Notes

  • あなたは (anata wa) is read “anata wa”, and えきへ いく (eki e iku) means “go to the station”. Notice how は and へ change sound in these.
  • は and ほ are close. ほ has an extra stroke, so compare them side by side.

How to practise

  • Say each sound out loud as you read it. Reading silently is much slower for building memory.
  • Write each new character about ten times on paper, following the stroke order from a reliable chart. Writing helps you notice the small differences between similar characters.
  • Cover the romaji column and try to read the characters, then check yourself. This is what the quiz at the end of every lesson does too.
