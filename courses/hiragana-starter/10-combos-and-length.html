Combined Sounds, Small っ and Long Vowels

Goal: Read combined sounds like きょ, double consonants with small っ, and long vowels.

Three last ideas

You now know every character. Three small rules fill in the rest of the picture: small ゃ ゅ ょ make combined sounds, small っ doubles a consonant, and long vowels last twice as long.

Combined sounds

How combos work

Small ゃ ゅ ょ blend into one syllable

き + ゃ makes きゃ, read “kya” as one beat. Compare ひや (hi-ya, two beats) with ひゃ (hya, one beat). The small size is what tells you to blend.

Spelling hints

しゃ しゅ しょ are “sha shu sho”, ちゃ ちゅ ちょ are “cha chu cho” and じゃ じゅ じょ are “ja ju jo”. They follow the spellings of し, ち and じ.

Small っ: a short pause

っ doubles the next consonant

A small っ is not a sound on its own. It tells you to hold the next consonant for an extra beat, like a tiny stop. きて (kite, “come”) vs きって (kitte, “stamp”), and がっこう (gakkou, “school”).

Long vowels

Stretch the vowel for two beats

あ, い, う after the same vowel makes it long: おかあさん (okaasan), おにいさん (oniisan), ゆうめい (yuumei).

え and お sounds

A long お sound is usually written with う, as in ありがとう and おとうさん, and sometimes with お, as in おおきい (ookii). A long え sound is usually written with い, as in せんせい (sensei).

Length changes meaning

おばさん (obasan, “aunt”) and おばあさん (obaasan, “grandmother”) differ only by one stretched vowel. Hold long vowels for twice as long.

How to practise

  • Say each sound out loud as you read it. Reading silently is much slower for building memory.
  • Write each new character about ten times on paper, following the stroke order from a reliable chart. Writing helps you notice the small differences between similar characters.
  • Cover the romaji column and try to read the characters, then check yourself. This is what the quiz at the end of every lesson does too.
