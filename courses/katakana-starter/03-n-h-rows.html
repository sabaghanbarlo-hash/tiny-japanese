The H row

Notes on the new shapes

ヘ is also a hiragana

ヘ (katakana) and へ (hiragana) look almost the same. Both are read “he”. You will find them in different words.

ニ is just two lines

ニ (ni) is two horizontal strokes, the top one shorter. It looks a lot like the kanji 二, which means “two”.

フ is read “fu”

Not “hu”. Like ふ in hiragana, the H row's third sound is between h and f, so it is spelt “fu”.

Words you can read now

  • コーヒーkoohiicoffee
  • ノートnootonotebook
  • ネクタイnekutainecktie
  • ナイフnaifuknife
  • ナイスnaisunice
  • ヒーターhiitaaheater
  • ヘアheahair
  • ホースhoosuhose

How to practise

  • Say each sound out loud as you read it. You already know the sounds from hiragana, so this is only about new shapes.
  • Write each new character about ten times, following a reliable stroke-order chart. Writing helps you notice the small differences between look-alike characters.
  • Read the words aloud and guess what they are before checking the meaning. Most katakana words come from English, so you will often get them right.
