The R Row, ワ, ヲ and ン

Goal: Read ラ リ ル レ ロ, ワ ヲ ン and words like ホテル, ラーメン and レストラン.

The R row

ワ, ヲ and ン

Notes

The Japanese R is a soft tap

It sits between English r, l and d. Touch the tip of your tongue quickly to the ridge behind your top teeth, as in the middle of “butter” in American English.

ン is a syllable of its own

ン (n) counts as one beat and only appears after other sounds, never at the start of a word. It sounds a bit like “m” before p, b or m, but you simply write ン.

ン and ソ, ワ and ウ

Compare ン (the long stroke sweeps up) with ソ (it sweeps down). ワ (wa) looks like ウ without the short stroke on top. These pairs are easy to mix up, so practise them side by side.

ヲ is rare

ヲ (o) is mostly used for the grammar particle in hiragana (を). In katakana it appears almost only in unusual cases. Learn to recognise it, and you are done.

That's all 46

You now know the whole basic katakana chart. Next: the little marks that give you the extra sounds, just like in hiragana.

How to practise

  • Say each sound out loud as you read it. You already know the sounds from hiragana, so this is only about new shapes.
  • Write each new character about ten times, following a reliable stroke-order chart. Writing helps you notice the small differences between look-alike characters.
  • Read the words aloud and guess what they are before checking the meaning. Most katakana words come from English, so you will often get them right.
