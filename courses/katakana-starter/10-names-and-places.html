Writing your own name

Sound it out, syllable by syllable

Say your name slowly and split it into Japanese beats. Alex becomes ア・レ・ッ・ク・ス: アレックス. Add ー for long vowels and ッ for doubled sounds.

There can be more than one version

Names like Emma can be written エマ or エンマ. Both are fine, because katakana spelling follows how a name sounds, not one fixed rule.

Try this

Write your own name in katakana on paper. Then try the names of three friends, your city and your favourite food. You are now using katakana the way Japanese people do.

How to practise

  • Say each sound out loud as you read it. You already know the sounds from hiragana, so this is only about new shapes.
  • Write each new character about ten times, following a reliable stroke-order chart. Writing helps you notice the small differences between look-alike characters.
  • Read the words aloud and guess what they are before checking the meaning. Most katakana words come from English, so you will often get them right.
