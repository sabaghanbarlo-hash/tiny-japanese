At the Bookstore

Goal: By the end you can ask how much something costs, say it is a little expensive, and ask for an item.

Alex and Mika compare book prices. The dialogue below is copied verbatim from the selected story script.

Content status: AI-written learning notes; Japanese and romaji have not been verified by a native speaker.

Dialogue

  • Alex
    すみません。この ほんは いくらですか？
    Sumimasen. Kono hon wa ikura desu ka?
    Excuse me. How much is this book?
  • Mika
    せんえんです。
    Sen'en desu.
    It's 1,000 yen.
  • Alex
    ちょっと たかいですね。
    Chotto takai desu ne.
    It's a little expensive, isn't it?
  • Mika
    あの ほんは やすいですよ。
    Ano hon wa yasui desu yo.
    That book is cheap!
  • Alex
    じゃあ、あの ほんを ください。
    Jaa, ano hon o kudasai.
    Then, that book, please.
  • Mika
    はい、どうぞ！
    Hai, douzo!
    Sure, here you go!

Grammar

この / その / あの + noun

このほん means “this book,” そのほん means “that book near the listener,” and あのほん means “that book over there, away from both people.” These words come directly before a noun.

Noun は いくらですか？

Use いくらですか to ask how much something costs: この ほんは いくらですか？ = “How much is this book?” は marks the topic.

ちょっと + adjective + ですね

ちょっとたかいですね means “It's a little expensive, isn't it?” ですね can soften a statement and invite the other person to agree.

よ and ね have different tones

よ presents information the speaker is telling the listener; ね often seeks shared agreement or a sense of “right?” Neither particle has one exact English equivalent.
