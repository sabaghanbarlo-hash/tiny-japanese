Lesson 4 · Ordering a drink

At the Café

Goal: By the end you can say “Coffee, please”, “I'd like coffee too”, and “You're welcome.”

Mika and Alex each ask for a coffee. You'll learn the polite pattern for asking for something.

Dialogue

  • Mika
    こんにちは！コーヒーを おねがいします。
    Konnichiwa! Koohii o onegaishimasu.
    Hello! Coffee, please.
  • Alex
    ぼくも コーヒーを おねがいします。
    Boku mo koohii o onegaishimasu.
    I'd like coffee too, please.
  • Mika
    ありがとうございます！
    Arigatou gozaimasu!
    Thank you!
  • Alex
    どういたしまして。
    Dou itashimashite.
    You're welcome!

Tip: untick Romaji and English to test yourself, then tick them to check.

Vocabulary

  • コーヒーkoohiicoffee
  • をoparticle that marks the thing you are asking for or acting on
  • お願いします (おねがいします)onegaishimasuplease / I'd like … (polite request)
  • 僕 (ぼく)bokuI / me (commonly used by men and boys)
  • もmoalso / too
  • どういたしましてdou itashimashiteyou're welcome

Grammar

Thing + を おねがいします — “…, please”

To ask for something politely, name it, add を, then おねがいします. コーヒーを おねがいします = “Coffee, please.” Swap in another word to ask for something else.

を is pronounced “o”

The particle を is written を but read “o”, which is why the romaji is koohii o onegaishimasu.

も = “too”

ぼくも コーヒーを … means “I'd like coffee too.” も replaces は, just like in Lesson 2.

Notes

  • ぼく is commonly used by men and boys. Many women use わたし instead, so choose the one that fits you.
  • Long vowels are written double in this site's romaji: koohii (コーヒー), ohayou, arigatou.
