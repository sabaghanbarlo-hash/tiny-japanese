Lesson 1 · Greetings & names

Meeting for the First Time

Goal: By the end you can greet someone, ask “What's your name?”, and say “I'm ___.”

Mika and Alex meet for the first time. It's a short, simple exchange you can use almost straight away.

Dialogue

  • Mika
    こんにちは！
    Konnichiwa!
    Hello!
  • Alex
    こんにちは！
    Konnichiwa!
    Hello!
  • Mika
    おなまえは？
    Onamae wa?
    What's your name?
  • Alex
    アレックスです。
    Arekkusu desu.
    I'm Alex.
  • Mika
    わたしは ミカです。
    Watashi wa Mika desu.
    I'm Mika.
  • Alex
    よろしくおねがいします！
    Yoroshiku onegaishimasu!
    Nice to meet you!

Tip: untick Romaji and English to test yourself, then tick them to check.

Vocabulary

  • こんにちはkonnichiwahello (used in the daytime)
  • お名前 (おなまえ)onamaename (polite; お is a polite prefix)
  • はwatopic particle: “as for …”
  • ですdesuis / am / are (polite)
  • 私 (わたし)watashiI / me
  • よろしくお願いします (よろしくおねがいします)yoroshiku onegaishimasunice to meet you (literally a polite request for a good relationship)

Grammar

X は Y です — “X is Y”

は marks what you are talking about, and です finishes the sentence politely. わたしは ミカです means “I am Mika.” When it's obvious who you mean, Japanese often drops the “I”, which is why Alex can simply say アレックスです.

おなまえは？ — a short question

Adding は to a word and raising your voice at the end turns it into “and your name?” — a friendly way to ask someone's name. You don't need か in this short form.

Pronunciation: は is “wa” here

The character は is normally “ha”, but as a particle it is pronounced “wa”. That's why わたしは is read watashi wa.

Notes

  • よろしくおねがいします is used when you meet someone, and also in other situations such as asking for a favour, so “Nice to meet you” is a good translation here but not the whole story.
