We Missed the Train!

Goal: By the end you can ask when the next train is, talk about time, and invite someone to do something.

Mika and Alex miss a train but make a new plan. The dialogue below is copied verbatim from the selected story script.

Content status: AI-written learning notes; Japanese and romaji have not been verified by a native speaker.

Dialogue

  • Alex
    ミカさん、いそいで！でんしゃが きます！
    Mika-san, isoide! Densha ga kimasu!
    Mika, hurry! The train is coming!
  • Mika
    えっ！まってください！
    E! Matte kudasai!
    What?! Wait for me!
  • Alex
    ああ！でんしゃに のれませんでした。
    Aa! Densha ni noremasen deshita.
    Oh no! We couldn't get on the train.
  • Mika
    だいじょうぶです。つぎの でんしゃは なんじですか？
    Daijoubu desu. Tsugi no densha wa nanji desu ka?
    It's okay. What time is the next train?
  • Alex
    ごご さんじです。
    Gogo sanji desu.
    It's at three p.m.
  • Mika
    まだ さんじまで じかんが ありますね。
    Mada sanji made jikan ga arimasu ne.
    We still have time until three, don't we?
  • Alex
    そうですね。コーヒーを のみませんか？
    Sou desu ne. Koohii o nomimasen ka?
    That's true. Would you like to have coffee?
  • Mika
    いいですね！
    Ii desu ne!
    Sounds good!

Grammar

Verb ます form + か for a polite question

つぎの でんしゃは なんじですか asks “What time is the next train?” か at the end makes a polite question.

まで = until / up to

さんじまで means “until three o'clock.” まだ さんじまで じかんが ありますね means there is still time before three.

〜ませんか to invite someone

コーヒーを のみませんか literally resembles “Won't you drink coffee?” but works as a polite invitation: “Would you like to have coffee?” を marks coffee as the object.

〜れませんでした: couldn't do something

のれませんでした is the past negative potential form of 乗る (のる, to ride/get on): “couldn't get on.” The train sentence uses に for the train you try to board.
