Lesson 2 · Introductions

What's Your Name?

Goal: By the end you can say “Nice to meet you”, ask “Are you a student?”, answer yes, and say “I'm a student too.”

Mika and Alex introduce themselves and find something they have in common.

Dialogue

  • Mika
    はじめまして！ミカです。
    Hajimemashite! Mika desu.
    Nice to meet you! I'm Mika.
  • Alex
    はじめまして。アレックスです。
    Hajimemashite. Arekkusu desu.
    Nice to meet you. I'm Alex.
  • Mika
    アレックスさんは、がくせいですか？
    Arekkusu-san wa, gakusei desu ka?
    Alex, are you a student?
  • Alex
    はい、がくせいです。
    Hai, gakusei desu.
    Yes, I'm a student.
  • Mika
    わたしも、がくせいです！
    Watashi mo, gakusei desu!
    I'm a student too!
  • Alex
    そうですか。よろしくおねがいします。
    Sou desu ka. Yoroshiku onegaishimasu.
    I see. Nice to meet you.
  • Mika
    こちらこそ、よろしくね！
    Kochira koso, yoroshiku ne!
    Likewise! Nice to meet you too!

Tip: untick Romaji and English to test yourself, then tick them to check.

Vocabulary

  • はじめましてhajimemashitenice to meet you (said when you first meet)
  • 〜さん-sanpolite title added after someone else's name
  • 学生 (がくせい)gakuseistudent
  • はいhaiyes
  • かkaquestion particle
  • もmoalso / too
  • そうですかsou desu kaI see / oh, is that so?
  • こちらこそkochira kosolikewise / same to you
  • ねnefriendly sentence ending, like “right?” or a soft “!”

Grammar

Add か to ask a question

がくせいです = “(I) am a student.” がくせいですか？ = “Are (you) a student?” か at the end turns a polite sentence into a question.

も instead of は means “too”

わたしは がくせいです = “I'm a student.” わたしも がくせいです = “I'm a student too.” も replaces は when the same thing is also true.

さん goes after other people's names

Add さん to another person's name to be polite: アレックスさん. Don't add it to your own name — Mika says ミカです, not ミカさんです.

ね sounds friendly

Mika ends with よろしくね, a warmer, more casual tone than よろしくおねがいします. It suits a friendly chat.

Notes

  • The published video writes がくせい in hiragana. The kanji form 学生 is shown in the vocabulary list so you can start to recognise it.
