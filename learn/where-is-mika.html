Episode 16 · Travel & everyday Japanese · Asking for directions

Where Is Mika?

Goal: By the end you can ask where something is, understand “next to,” say you got lost, and suggest going together.

Alex loses track of Mika in a shopping mall. The dialogue below is copied verbatim from the selected story script.

Content status: AI-written learning notes; Japanese and romaji have not been verified by a native speaker.

Dialogue

  • Alex
    あれ？ミカさんは どこですか？
    Are? Mika-san wa doko desu ka?
    Huh? Where is Mika?
  • Alex
    すみません。トイレは どこですか？
    Sumimasen. Toire wa doko desu ka?
    Excuse me. Where is the restroom?
  • Mika
    トイレは あの かいだんの となりですよ！
    Toire wa ano kaidan no tonari desu yo!
    The restroom is next to those stairs!
  • Alex
    あっ、ミカさん！どこに いましたか？
    A, Mika-san! Doko ni imashita ka?
    Oh, Mika! Where were you?
  • Mika
    ここに いましたよ！ずっと まっていました。
    Koko ni imashita yo! Zutto matte imashita.
    I was right here! I've been waiting the whole time.
  • Alex
    ごめんなさい！みちに まよいました。
    Gomennasai! Michi ni mayoimashita.
    I'm sorry! I got lost.
  • Mika
    だいじょうぶです。いっしょに いきましょう！
    Daijoubu desu. Issho ni ikimashou!
    It's okay. Let's go together!

Grammar

X は どこですか？

Use a place or person + は どこですか to ask where it is: トイレは どこですか？ = “Where is the restroom?”

あの + noun + の + 隣

あの かいだんの となり means “next to those stairs over there.” の connects the noun かいだん (stairs) to となり (next to/beside).

いました and 〜ていました

いました is the polite past of います, used for people and animals. まっていました (待っていました) means “was waiting” and can be rendered “had been waiting” depending on context.

いきましょう = let's go

The 〜ましょう form makes a suggestion or invitation: いきましょう = “Let's go.” に in ここに いました marks where Mika was; in みちに まよいました, the particle is part of the expression for getting lost.

Notes

  • The source script spells dialogue words mostly in hiragana; kanji are shown in vocabulary only to help recognition. Dialogue is unchanged.
  • The romaji Are? for あれ？ is a rough learner rendering of a surprised interjection; pronunciation and the English nuance should be confirmed by a native speaker.
